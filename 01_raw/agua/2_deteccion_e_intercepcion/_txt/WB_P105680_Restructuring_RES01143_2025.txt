Project Development Objective (DO) 
Current Development Objective
The overall development objective of the proposed APL program supports the Government's Integrated Basin Cleanup 
while simultaneously improving sanitary conditions along the banks of La Plata River and providing a long-term and 
cost-effective solution for safe disposal of wastewater from the Buenos Aires Metropolitan Area (AySAs concession 
area). The two stage APL program that contributes to this objective and the allocation of works and activities under 
each APL has been specifically designed to ensure that APL-1 can be free-standing, with no stranded assets at the end 
of the first stage.

sewerage services in the M-R River Basin and other parts of the Province and City of Buenos Aires by expanding

I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the Regional Vice-President of a Level Two Restructuring for an 
extension of the closing date of the Argentina Matanza-Riachuelo Basin Sustainable Development Project (P105680). 
This is the seventh restructuring and fifth extension of the Project and includes (i) extending the Project closing date by 
6 months from September 30, 2025, to March 31, 2026, and the accompanying changes to this extension: (ii) adjusting 
the results framework, (iii) updating disbursement estimates, and (iv) revising the implementation schedule. The 
proposed restructuring is necessary to finalize key infrastructure works allowing Bank's safeguards policies compliance 
and orderly closing of the Project.
2. The Project consists of three IBRD loans, totaling US$1.228 billion - for a total project cost, including counterpart 
funding, of US$1.483 billion. The initial financing of US$840 million (IBRD Loan 7706), was approved by the Board of 
Executive Directors on June 9, 2009, and became effective on July 20, 2010. A first Additional Financing (AF) for US$245 
million (IBRD Loan 9008) was approved on September 5, 2019. A second AF of US$265 million (IBRD Loan 9252) was 
approved on June 1, 2021. The Project comprises four components: enhancing sewerage services (Component 1), 
reducing industrial wastewater discharges (Component 2), improving environmental territorial management 
(Component 3), and strengthening institutions (Component 4). 
A. OVERALL PROJECT STATUS
3. The Project is currently rated as Moderately Satisfactory for both implementation progress and advancement 
towards its development objective, with three out of five indicators met. The completion of the Riachuelo Wastewater 
Treatment Plant (WWTP) and the entire Riachuelo System under Component 1 is scheduled for the end of August 2025; 
this is expected to enable achievement of the fourth indicator-volume of sewage adequately treated-within the first 
months of operation. The fifth indicator, concerning reduction of industrial pollution, depends on the finalization and 
operation of the industrial WWTP (PTELI for its Spanish acronym) contract under Component 2.
4. Although the two key infrastructures under construction (Riachuelo WWTP and PTELI) have suffered delays, the 
Project has reached important milestones. Currently, the Riachuelo System (Lots 1, 2, and 3) under Component 1 is 
being tested with sewage effluent, under conditions similar to the system's final operational state. This system is already 
showing important benefits, decreasing the pressure on the existing sewerage network and avoiding overflows. Under 
Component 2, current investments will help manage pollution discharges from the tannery sector, a major source of 
industrial wastewater in the basin. The Tannery Industrial Park (PIC, due to its Spanish acronym) was finalized in October 
2023, and PTELI’s physical implementation progress was at 90 percent as of June 30, 2025, with works expected to be 
completed by March 2026. ACUMAR has been working closely with the tannery association on the development of the 
operating regulations and processes needed to enable the operation of the PIC. 
5. High government commitment and disbursement reflect the advanced status of implementation, but the 
restricted budget space and past difficulties with accessing foreign currency have affected implementation. The three 
loans are fully committed, with an overall disbursement rate of 99 percent (US$1.2 billion). However, the on-going 
implementation of Argentina’s stabilization program has limited the allocation of budget to public investments, thus 
negatively impacting the timeliness and funding needs, which have prevented the Riachuelo WWTP contract under

6. Financial Management (FM) and Procurement are rated as Moderately Satisfactory. The Project Implementation

7. The Project is in full compliance with its Environmental and Social (E&S) Safeguards instruments. The Project

established E&S management supervision scheme for the whole project. The Bank’s team will continue to closely monitor 
the remaining works throughout the extension, ensuring safeguards' requirements are successfully completed before 
loan closure. 
B. RATIONALE FOR PROJECT RESTRUCTURING
8. A six-month extension is expected to be sufficient to ensure satisfactory completion of the Project. The 
extension will cover the initial operation of the Riachuelo system, allowing the achievement of the fourth PDO indicator. 
Additionally, it will cover the finalization of the construction of PTELI (excluding the operation of the plant). The extension 
will compensate for the delays incurred since the beginning of the implementation of the stabilization program. The 
Borrower has expressed its commitment to finalizing the activities.
9. The six-month extension will enable complete key construction to reduce industrial pollution in the basin and 
allow orderly closure of the Project, including complying with safeguard requirements (as per the project's EIA/ESMP) 
and without the need to prepare and monitor an E&S commitment plan (ESCP) after loan closure. Although the 
extension will only cover the conclusion of PTELI’s construction (excluding the operation of the plant), it will support the 
design of a sustainability plan to ensure the long-term sustainability of this infrastructure by minimizing any potential 
E&S Risk. The full operation of PTELI will take several years as it involves voluntary relocation of multiple small and midsize 
tanneries. The extension will allow for completion of civil works, and also the development of a sustainability plan to 
include, among others, agreement on the discharging point (formalizing the connection with an existing WWTP managed 
by the local utility, AySA), the transfer of medium and small tanneries to the PIC, and connections with neighboring 
leather companies, following the relevant World Bank Environmental, Health, and Safety Guidelines. The sustainability 
plan will also include an E&S screening and E&S requirements for design and completion of the pending activities. The 
extension is needed to ensure continued monitoring and support for Project activities through to full completion, in 
compliance with the World Bank’s safeguards instruments.
II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES
10. Extend the loan closing date. The Project closing date will be extended by 6 months from September 30, 2025, to 
March 31, 2026, to allow the completion of ongoing key contracts and activities, as well as an orderly closing of the 
Project. This would be the fifth extension of the closing date. The Borrower’s performance is satisfactory considering the 
great advance of this very complex project. The Borrower and the Bank have agreed on an action plan to complete the 
Project. The Borrower has committed to completing the Project.
11. Accompanying adjustments to the loan closing date. The restructuring will also adjust the Results Framework to 
reflect the new closing date for the achievement of the end targets; the disbursement estimates to ensure that
