I. 
STRATEGIC CONTEXT 
 
A. 
Country Context 
1. 
The COVID-19 outbreak has hit Argentina´s economy at a moment of significant macroeconomic 
imbalances and social stress. Following a two-year recession, high inflation, and lack of access to capital markets, the 
strict lockdown to contain the spread of the pandemic triggered the sharpest Gross Domestic Product (GDP) decline 
ever recorded, in March-April of 2020.  The gradual easing of confinement measures has allowed economic activity 
to slowly pick up pace since the third quarter but has also set off a rapid rise in COVID-19 cases. The implementation 
of a fiscal stimulus package – worth 3.5 percent of GDP1 - coupled with an abrupt decline in revenues resulted in a 
central government deficit of nearly 7 percent of GDP in 20202, the largest in more than 30 years. Lack of market 
access mandated a full monetization of the deficit, which is exacerbating macroeconomic imbalances. The gap 
between official and parallel foreign exchange rates is at historic high levels and the Central Bank has rapidly lost 
reserves as it attempted to maintain the official exchange rate stable, despite a trade surplus and tight currency 
controls.  
2. 
It is estimated that the economy has declined by over 10 percent in 20203 and the unprecedented 
economic contraction has had a severe impact on employment and poverty. Economic recovery is gradually picking 
up as containment measures are progressively lifted, building on the economy’s ample idle capacity. However, high 
uncertainty, price and capital controls limit investment growth, while the imperative to reduce the large fiscal deficit 
will strongly curtail demand stimulus. As a result, only a partial rebound is expected in 2021, and the economy is not 
projected to reach 2019 GDP levels before 2023. Labor market statistics point to a sharp 9 percentage points decline 
in formal employment between the first and the second quarter to 33.4 percent, with a partial recovery in the third 
quarter to 37.4 percent. Unemployment has increased only by 2.5 percentage points in the second quarter as an 
important share of workers ceased looking for jobs and receded to 11.7 in the third quarter4.   
3. 
As a result of higher inactivity rates and decreasing purchasing power, Argentina’s poverty rate increased 
from 35 to 41 percent in the first half of FY2020, with 11.7 million people now living below the poverty line and 3 
million in extreme poverty.5  More than half of Argentina’s poor live in Greater Buenos Aires (Gran Buenos Aires, 
GBA), by far the greatest concentration of urban and peri-urban poverty in the Southern Cone of Latin America.6 With 
15.4 million inhabitants, GBA is one of Latin America’s urban giants, accounting for one third of Argentina’s population 
and nearly half of its GDP.7 Although urbanization has contributed to economic growth and improved living standards 
for millions of people, it has not always been accompanied by adequate planning and investment in infrastructure 
and services, leading to “pockets of poverty.” At the close of second semester 2019, the percentage of people living 
in poverty and indigence in GBA was already considerable, standing at 35.2 and 9.3 percent, respectively. The 
outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic raised these figures to 41.6 percent and 11.7 percent.8

4. 
In GBA, which is ground zero for the COVID-19 pandemic in Argentina, low-income neighborhoods are 
being hit the hardest by the sanitary and economic crises. There are three main factors driving the high vulnerability 
of GBA’s poor: crowded living conditions; high dependence on informal economic activities, which undermines 
compliance with lockdown policies; and lack of access to safely managed water supply and sanitation (WSS), all of 
which are key to preventing virus transmission. Over 30 percent of these neighborhoods are in flood-prone areas, 
rendering existing informal and often unsafe WSS systems even more prone to failure.9 Furthermore, a methodology 
prepared by the World Bank to predict contagion hotspots for COVID-19 in cities uses access to WSS and population 
density as the main identification criteria10.  
B.              Sectoral and Institutional Context 
Water Supply and Sanitation Sector Performance Challenges at the National Level 
5. 
Argentina faces a considerable gap in access to piped water, sewerage and wastewater treatment. An 
estimated 7.5 million inhabitants (17 percent of the population) lack access to safely managed piped water systems, 
and 21 million (48 percent) are not connected to sewerage networks. The combined economic impact of these service 
gaps is estimated at 1.32 percent of GDP,11 including harm to health and education — the essentials for building 
Argentina´s human capital. Furthermore, only 11 percent of total wastewater generated is treated12, which poses 
serious environmental and health impacts, especially on populations living near polluted urban waterways.

6. 
Although the main provincial WSS operators provide acceptable service levels in terms of continuity and 
water quality, their efficiency is low compared with regional peers.13 Critical indicators are the low level of micro-
metering (25 percent compared to 80 percent average for main regional utilities) and the high level of daily per capita 
water production (560 liters compared to a regional average of 330 liters and an international reference value of 200 
liters). Without micro-metering, real volumetric tariffs cannot be applied, making it difficult to link operating costs 
and tariff revenues and to control physical and commercial losses (estimated at 45 percent compared to 36 percent 
average for main regional utilities). The financial sustainability of Argentina’s main WSS service providers is also low. 
On average, tariff revenues cover 83 percent5 of operation and maintenance (O&M) costs, with the rest provided as 
subsidies by the provinces and, in the case of the GBA operator, Agua y Saneamientos Argentinos S.A. (AySA), by the 
National Government.

the management of water resources and provision of water services. However, the National Government is 
responsible for guaranteeing the fulfillment of national objectives related to socio-economic development and 
environmental protection – where water plays a key role. The national objectives laid out in the first National Plan for

Sectoral Context in GBA 
8. 
AySA is the concessionaire responsible for providing WSS services to most of GBA14. AySA is one of the 
largest WSS utilities in the world, and the largest in the country, with 14.4 million residents (96 percent of the 
population in GBA) living in its service area. It is estimated that 66 percent of the population without access to piped 
water in Argentina (3.7 million) and 39 percent without access to sewerage (6.8 million), live in this area. The 
concession area is divided into two clearly differentiated zones, the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires – with high 
levels of piped water and sewerage coverage - and the municipalities and peri-urban communities of the Province of 
Buenos Aires (PBA) with lower service coverage (as described in Table 1).  
9. 
The regulatory and institutional frameworks at AySA´s level are well established, although they still 
present some challenges. AySA is 90 percent owned by the National Government through the Ministry of Public 
Works, which is responsible for tariff setting, and 10 percent by its 7,800 employees through the “Participatory 
Ownership Program”. The WSS Regulator (Ente Regulador de Agua y Saneamiento, ERAS) is an experienced 
autonomous public body created in 1993 and as such, it has gained important experience in asset management, 
service quality monitoring and development of tariff models. Moreover, ERAS has incorporated into its decision-
making process two committees representing municipalities in its concession area and civil society organizations 
representing the users. ERAS is also responsible for monitoring AySA's compliance with its legal obligations under the 
concession contract. The National Planning Agency (Agencia de Planificación, APLA) is an autonomous public body in 
charge of reviewing and coordinating investments in expansion and service improvement carried out by AySA. The 
main challenges facing the regulation of WSS service in GBA relate to the need to adapt regulation to the service 
delivery model of a state-owned company, strengthen capacities in these entities, and establish a clear tariff setting 
methodology.  
AySA’s Specific Challenges 
10.

Since 2006, AySA has focused considerable attention on expanding WSS services in the peri-urban

Untreated wastewater continues to pose significant environmental and health concerns. According to 
AySA, the utility has a capacity to treat 2.8 million m3/day of wastewater, corresponding to the share of the population 
connected to sewerage. Of the treated effluent, only 26 percent of the wastewater receives secondary treatment.

more than 6 million people not connected to sewers are often inadequately disposed, polluting aquifers or discharged 
in urban waterways, creating public health concerns for those living along the riverbanks. 
15. 
The risk to public health is compounded by floods of increasing frequency and intensity due to climate 
change. During the period of 2000-2011, flooding events affected 5.5 million people and caused nearly US$4.5 billion 
in losses. Although this trend is reversing due to different interventions being implemented, the risk remains 
significant, particularly in peri-urban areas.25 More than one million people in the metropolitan area of Buenos Aires 
are exposed to floods with a return period of 5 years, and more than 30 percent of fragile neighborhoods are exposed 
to 100-year return period floods.26   
16. 
Public health concerns can also be traced to a widescale lack of sewerage connections and the use of 
unsafe groundwater. Numerous peri-urban areas use groundwater rendered unsuitable to serve as a sole drinking 
water source due to years of unchecked pollution – primarily associated with households relying on suboptimal onsite 
sanitation solutions. As a result, piped water tapping into groundwater sources sometimes surpass national regulatory 
limits on nitrate concentrations. Such is the case for the estimated 100,000 residents of Florencio Varela, most of 
whom are vulnerable, who are exposed to unsafe water, underscoring the urgency to increase the quality and safety 
of water services in this area. Similarly, there is a pressing need for AySA to provide residents of the Escobar, Esteban 
Echeverría, Ezeiza, San Fernando, San Isidro, San Martin, and Tigre municipalities access to safely managed sanitation 
services by ensuring sewage treatment and that associated discharges are in compliance with regulatory standards.

infrastructure investment needs, were financed by short-term debt and transfers from the National Government 
(US$450 million), equivalent to 80 percent of AySA’s total revenues.27

Under Results Area 1.1, the Program will expand safely managed water supply services in selected 
municipalities of peri-urban Buenos Aires. Approximately 140,000 people will benefit from the activities under this 
sub-result area, of which 100,00051 are located in the municipality of Florencio Varela. This area is characterized by 
water quality problems due to the presence of nitrates in the groundwater source which feeds the distribution 
network. Under the Program, AySA aims to improve the quality of water provided to the residents of this municipality 
by bringing treated surface water sourced from the Belgrano water treatment plant (WTP) to the area and mixing it 
with existing groundwater, thereby lowering nitrate concentrations to safe drinking standards. This action will 
furthermore reduce the energy requirements related to groundwater pumping. To this end, AySA plans to build a new 
4,000 m3/h pumping station, a 1,600 m long and 1200 mm diameter reinforcement pipeline, and a 4,550 m long and 
700 mm diameter conveyance pipeline to the “Cruce” cistern, where the clean water will be mixed with existing 
groundwater. Of the total beneficiaries from these interventions, 14,500 people live in fragile neighborhoods, where 
household connections already exist.

professionals selected to execute and supervise the works.

these activities. The PPE will be reinforced by capturing the lessons learnt from dealing with the ongoing

Under Results Area 3.3 AySA will promote gender diversity and inclusion. As one of the largest utilities in

for gender diversity in promotions, particularly to management positions. AySA has found that strong gender norms 
on the type of work that is considered appropriate for women tend to deter them from applying to technical and 
managerial positions. For this reason, it is launching targeted communications campaigns to motivate women to apply 
to such opportunities, and also offering targeted training to fill existing capacity gaps in women’s existing technical 
and leadership skills. Through these actions, AySA aims to increase the percentage of women in managerial positions. 
Given the large number of employees, and taking into account the current recruitment rates, as well as new 
promotions targets, the utility intends to achieve a gradual but steady increase in women’s representation in 
managerial positions – from 22 to 28 percent – which in turn, is expected to  contribute to improving AySA’s  capacity 
to  respond to the differential impacts and needs of women and men in its emergency response efforts.

IV. 
ASSESSMENT SUMMARY 
 
A. 
Technical (including program economic evaluation) 
77. 
The Program’s strategic relevance is high, as it makes clear contributions to key areas of AySA´s core 
expansion and improvement plans as laid out in the PMOEM. The Program is aligned with the PMOEM’s three Sub-
programs and focuses on essential objectives at the core of AySA´s mission, that is, to speed up the provision of safely 
managed WSS services in a sustainable and efficient manner to the peri-urban areas of Buenos Aires, where a large 
share of the population lives under the poverty line. The Program acquires even greater relevance in the context of 
the COVID-19 pandemic, where the impacts of the lack of access to safe water and sanitation in vulnerable 
neighborhoods have become more evident than ever, as these are basic barriers needed to slow down the spread of 
the virus.   
78. 
From a long-term perspective, the Program is also designed to increase the operational and financial 
sustainability of AySA in order to decrease its dependence on federal budget transfers. The results sought by the 
Program will serve as steps in the right direction in a challenging macro-economic context. Setting up solid paths to 
reduce large water leaks and energy consumption, and to digitalize payment methods will reduce costs and increase 
revenues, and thereby help AySA climb the financial sustainability ladder, allowing it to better utilize subsidies to 
expand services faster to those who most need them. Moreover, the Program also supports essential modernization 
processes to make the company more agile in its capacity to respond to shocks.  
79. 
The technical soundness of the Program is considered solid. The selection of activities to achieve expected 
results is adequate. AySA has a long track record of good operation and maintenance of facilities, giving no reason to 
consider substantial risks in that regard. Moreover, the designs of infrastructure will take into account adaptation 
measures to limit climate-related risk.67 
80. 
The A+T and C+T programs have long track records of building networks and connecting households in 
informal settlements. AySA’s proposed evaluation of the potential beneficiaries is comprehensive as it addresses 
structural and socio-demographic aspects of the population. The nine pre-defined water and sanitation technical 
connection typologies are appropriate for informal settlements and ensure that the completed infrastructure will 
comply with existing regulations. An estimated 1,200 workers are expected to be employed in 30 cooperatives in low-
income neighborhoods, which is well justified in the context of post COVID-19 economic recovery. Importantly, AySA 
will provide training to the cooperatives to ensure they are equipped to handle the socio-technical features of the 
program, including consideration of gender aspects. 
81. 
It is not uncommon to find households in these informal settlements that are located in flood-prone 
areas. This risk will be assessed carefully before providing formal services. This consideration will be included in the 
DLI verification criteria, which will not recognize household connections in flood-prone areas, unless adequate flood 
mitigation plans are adopted. This will also incentivize AySA to build connections in climate-safer areas contributing 
to the adaptation agenda. 
82. 
The Bank assessed the technical designs for the optimization and extension of the two existing Jagüel 
and Norte WWTPs and determined that the proposed technology (activated sludge process) and the quality of the 
designs is adequate, confirming the proposed modality of contract (works) and the proposed 2-year construction 
timeline. However, AySA may be able to further optimize designs by performing a more rigorous analysis of 
technological alternatives during the preparation stage.

CoV-2” of July 2020. AySA implemented technical, commercial and organizational measures to protect staff while

lessons from the current emergency.

Internal Rate of Return (EIRR) of 40 percent. A cost benefit analysis was carried out and the costs were estimated at 
economic prices (market prices were adjusted with shadow price coefficients). Results Area 1 shows the highest 
benefits, which can be partially explained by existing sunk costs from prior investments in the network expansion. The 
benefits of the expansion projects are also considerable because they benefit densely populated areas with significant 
environmental problems. The beneficiary areas are low-income compared to AySA’s area of coverage and these users, 
relative to their income, value the water service more highly. 
96. 
Despite the conservative assumptions used for benefit estimation, projects in Results Area 2 have an 
economic rate of return above the discount rate. In addition to the quantified benefits in terms of savings in water 
production, energy consumption and system maintenance, other benefits include an increase in the useful life of the 
assets, costs savings from avoiding reactive maintenance and the reduction of interventions in public roads due to 
water losses. Given the current levels of operational efficiency, these projects are expected to strengthen AySA’s 
financial sustainability in the long term due to operational cost savings leading to a reduction in its dependence on 
subsidies.  
97. 
A GHG analysis was carried out on the Program’s expansion of sewerage service through the rehabilitation 
and renovation of Planta Norte y Jagüel WWTPs. The improvement in NRW and energy efficiency actions in networks, 
will lead to reductions in GHG emissions due to efficiency gains. The NPV of the Program includes US$66 million from 
GHG emissions avoided.  
98. 
 The results of the Program’s financial analysis reveal a positive NPV of US$88.4 million, largely driven by 
the operational efficiency improvements and the NRW reductions supported by the Program. This analysis takes 
AySA’s latest financial statement as a baseline and projects future cashflow, taking into account the expected change 
in revenue and costs resulting from the Program’s implementation. It considers operating flows since capital costs are 
directly subsidized by the National Government through transfers.

C. 
Environmental and Social 
105. 
The overall environmental and social impact of the Program is expected to be positive. The Program’s 
Environmental and Social System Assessment (ESSA) found that no significant adverse environmental impacts are 
expected as a result of the proposed interventions under the Program to support the increase of the supply and quality 
of drinking water and the expansion of sanitation services in targeted areas. The main positive environmental effects 
are related to the adequate collection, conduction, treatment and final disposal of a greater volume of sewage, with 
the consequent reduction of soil and groundwater contamination; the improvement of health conditions for the new 
population served; the environmental enhancement of the intervention areas from the blocking of septic tanks; 
among others. The reduction of water losses and energy consumption also entails environmental benefits. The 
Program would likely have overall positive social impacts on the lives and livelihoods of large vulnerable communities 
through the expansion of safety managed water and sanitation services, which will also improve the quality of life and 
reduce WASH-related health risks in targeted neighborhoods. The Program will also likely increase local employment 
through participation in cooperatives, thus generating income opportunities and promoting social inclusion. The 
Program will also support AySA’s citizen engagement and beneficiary feedback mechanisms, which will help to 
improve the quality of the service, and AySA’s readiness for emergency response. 
106. 
Environmental risks. The overall environmental risks and potential impacts of the Program are considered 
Substantial, basically in connection with interventions in existing WWTPs given the risk and potential impacts 
associated to civil works in the construction stage (soil movements; materials transportation and handling; noise, 
dust, debris generation; among others) and, once treatment capacity is expanded, to the increased volume of treated 
effluents to be discharged to water bodies during the operation stage. However, the proposed works are of a limited 
scale, will increase the level of treatment of effluents, and will take place within AySA's premises. Furthermore, they 
will not involve new discharge pipelines or discharge points, which means that the risks and potential environmental 
impacts to the water bodies where the treated effluents are discharged have been taken into account and, 
accordingly, evaluated in the original Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs) of the WWTPs. The original EIAs 
considered the modular design of the plants (i.e., to be built in stages) and the maximum volume of effluents once all 
modules are constructed and fully operational. The original EIAs will be updated and presented to the environmental 
authorities, as part of the renewal of the environmental licenses. The main specific risks that would need to be 
managed under the Program include: (i) increased health risks due to worker and community exposure to COVID-19; 
(ii) community and occupational health and safety risks – especially during construction and particularly associated

National / local / AySA’s system to address environmental and social risks. The ESSA concluded that 
Argentina has well-established systems to address these environmental, health and safety risks, including several 
strong central, provincial and municipal level legislations and institutions, as well as an effective regulatory sectoral 
framework that govern environmental, occupational and community health & safety issues. These are complemented 
by AySA´s corporate environmental, social and health & safety systems. In particular, AySA, through its Directorate 
for Sustainability, has developed Environmental and Social Technical Specifications (Especificaciones Técnicas 
Ambientales y Sociales, ETAS) that are part of the bidding documents and are included in the contracts once the works 
are awarded. The ETAS establish good practice standards and procedures as requirements for the contractors to 
address environmental and social issues during the execution of the works. Also, AySA has developed a series of 
documents regarding Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) following Argentina’s sound legal framework on the 
matter, which details the requirements for the contractors including the need for a risk assessment of their tasks and 
the development of a risk management plan. Specific measures recommended in the ESSA to manage identified 
environmental risks under the Program include: (i) ensuring the implementation of the Argentinian COVID-19 related 
regulations and corresponding AySA protocols for contracted workers and own staff, following the evolving pandemic 
and post-pandemic situations; (ii) ensuring the implementation of the national regulation on health and safety and 
the corresponding AySA´s systems, improving them for the proper handling of materials and waste with asbestos 
content (including potential chance findings during excavation and demolition); and, (iii) strengthening AySA´s risk 
management related to physical cultural resources and sensitive areas located within the urban zone of intervention. 
Specific measures to manage social risks include: (i) the adaptation of practices and procedures to the realities of low 
income neighborhoods to guarantee their sustainable access to WSS services (e.g. through the implementation of 
differentiated programs); (ii) the incorporation of processes to handle potential impact of works on community 
services and/or informal economic activities in the Program’s ETAS; and (iii) the active involvement of municipal 
government environmental and social teams.

Climate Change.  Buenos Aires metropolitan area is naturally exposed to floods, which may increase in 
frequency and intensity due to climate change. Vulnerability levels in low-income neighborhoods are compounded by 
different factors, including exposure to flood events, which can cause significant disruptions in the form of loss of 
assets and well-being. By providing access to safely managed water and sanitation services, and incentivizing the 
adoption of flood mitigation plans in flood prone areas, the Program will contribute to reduce overall vulnerability 
levels in these neighborhoods. In addition, AySA’s main assets are resilient, as the utility has clear criteria on flood-
proof engineering designs to protect the infrastructure both during the construction and the operation phases. The 
digitalization activities proposed will further increase AySA’s adaptive capacity through improved network monitoring 
and analysis and operational flexibility. From the operational perspective, the energy efficiency and reduction of NRW 
activities will also lead to GHG mitigation outcomes that will contribute to Argentina´s national climate agenda.

ANNEX 3. TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT SUMMARY 
 
A. 
OVERALL RATIONALE FOR THE PROGRAM 
1. 
The Program was designed to increase access to WSS services in vulnerable areas of AySA´s jurisdiction, 
while at the same time increase AySA´s resilience in times of the COVID-19 pandemic, as well as its efficiency, 
contributing to reducing its dependency on federal budget transfers and increasing its financial sustainability. By 
designing a strong system of incentives through well-defined DLIs, the Program will allow the achievement of 
challenging results such as expanding connectivity in the poorest areas through community participation, reducing 
water losses and energy consumption, increasing revenues, streamlining managerial processes and speeding up the 
agility of internal procedures, as well as promoting innovation and transparency. Moreover, the PforR will acknowledge 
AySA’s ongoing activities to mitigate the shocks derived from the COVID-19 crisis by strengthening the government’s 
and AySA’s own systems to recover in a timely manner, as well as prepare for other potential shocks. The Program is 
well aligned with the government’s commitment to prioritize investments in water and sanitation services, with 
particular focus on vulnerable areas. 
B. 
TECHNICAL SOUNDNESS 
Results Area 1: Expansion of WSS services with a focus on vulnerable areas  
(A) 
Household connections in fragile neighborhoods  
2. 
AySA will increase access to safely managed WSS services to households (HH) that, despite their proximity 
to already installed secondary networks, have not yet connected. Concurrently, this activity will promote local 
employment by implementing the corresponding works through local cooperatives, and will support and deepen social 
intervention strategies that communicate socio-environmental and technical information to encourage HH to connect 
to and care for WSS networks. Through the Program, AySA will build on the A+T and C+T models to deliver 10,000 intra-
HH water connections and 20,000 intra-HH sewerage connections, respectively. Within the eligible municipalities, the 
Program will target beneficiaries in fragile neighborhoods identified under this Program as those falling within the 
following classified categories by AySA: (i) ABC; (ii) UREM; and (iii) RENABAP. These areas were selected based on their 
sanitary and socio-economic conditions. Fragile neighborhoods are usually located in areas with complex terrain 
conditions: flood-prone land, high levels of water tables and limited access to basic services and health infrastructure.  
3. 
AySA’s proposed evaluation of the potential beneficiaries is robust as it addresses the population’s structural 
and sociodemographic aspects. From a technical perspective, the proposed rate of installation of 100 water or 36 
sewerage connections per cooperative per month is reasonable based on similar previous pilots in vulnerable areas 
and the nine pre-defined water and sanitation technical connection typologies are appropriate for the households to 
benefit from these interventions. Finally, A+T and C+T also have a long track record of mobilizing local workers and 
promoting jobs in low-income neighborhoods, which is well justified in the context of a post COVID-19 economic 
recovery phase. 
(B) 
Expansion of primary infrastructure – Florencio Varela (water), Norte and Jagüel (wastewater)  
Water Supply Infrastructure  
4. 
AySA will provide safely managed water services to the municipality of Florencia Varela, by reducing the 
amount of water coming from existing deep wells, which is of low quality, and substituting it with treated surface 
water from the Belgrano WTP. The number of beneficiaries is estimated at 100,000 (equivalent to 25,000 HH) of which 
14,500 live in fragile areas. Beneficiaries in this area already have household connections. The proposed solution is a 
technically reasonable approach to reduce the concentration of nitrates in water down to safe levels as per drinking 
water quality standards. Investments include civil works at the pumping station of 4000 m3/s capacity and the initial

Wastewater Infrastructure  
5. 
AySA will build wastewater infrastructure to serve 225,000 people at the end of the Program timeline. 
Beneficiaries from these interventions are in the municipalities of Escobar, Esteban Echeverría, Ezeiza, San Fernando, 
San Isidro, San Martin, and Tigre through infrastructure investments in wastewater treatment modules in existing 
WWTPs, extension of sewerage networks, including wastewater pumping stations, and household connections to be 
implemented through regular construction contracts. 
(i) 
The Norte WWTP – which receives wastewater from the networks of the Tigre, San Fernando and part 
of San Isidro districts – has two existing parallel treatment modules that treat 0.9 m3/s each, using the 
activated sludge process. The objective is the optimization of these two modules to first reach a total 
capacity of 2.16 m3/s, and the construction of a third module with a capacity of 1.0 m3/s. The total 
capacity of 3.16 m3/s is expected to be reached in 2030 and is equivalent to serve circa 1 million people.  
(ii) The Jagüel WWTP located in the Esteban Echeverría district within the Matanza Riachuelo Basin has two 
modules with different processes and treatment capacities. Module I includes pre-treatment, biological 
treatment by the activated sludge process (extended aeration), clarifiers, as well as sludge concentration 
and drying facilities. It can treat the wastewater of up to 30,000 people but is currently out of service. 
Module II includes pre-treatment, primary and secondary biological treatment through an attached-
growth process (trickling filters), as well as sludge concentration, digestion and drying facilities and has 
a capacity for serving 150,000 people (0.46m3/s). The project foresees an expansion of Jagüel, Module I 
to serve 100,000 people (0.36m3 /s), reaching the total capacity of the plant of 250,000 people or 
0.82m3/s) 
(iii) The expansion of the sewerage network is done through traditionally used materials such as PVC. A total 
of 410 km of pipe will be installed and connected to the Norte WWTP network, as well as 83 km 
(including 5km for the main collector) for the Jagüel WWTP, using pipejacking, a commonly used 
technique for installing underground pipelines and ducts. AySA has a policy of not allowing connections 
of HH to sewerage networks until a functioning WWTP can treat the collected sewage, and it will provide 
users with access to safely managed sanitation services  
6. 
The need for expansion of these WWTPs is justified by the planned expansion of the sewage collection 
networks, some of which is already ongoing. The proposed treatment process is adequate to ensure compliance with 
more stringent discharge standards to be enforced in the near future.  
7. 
Technical engineering designs regarding large Program infrastructure prepared by AySA are solid. Notably 
both WWTPs are designed to incorporate flood protection, with an average elevation of 0.5 m on top of the 100-year 
flood water levels, increasing infrastructure resilience. Bidding documents are sound and present detailed 
specifications of works and installations. Along the same line, the 2-year construction timeline for this type of 
technology is considered to be reasonable (not considering procurement). The technology selected is considered to be 
standard and if properly designed, constructed and operated, the WWTPs will present high removal efficiencies of 
pollutants. Works will be contracted using a "turnkey" modality with detailed engineering designs and construction 
under the contractors’ responsibility, ensuring a smooth transition towards the operational phase. A series of aspects 
where efficiency in the design could be improved have been identified by this assessment and included in the 
recommendations section.

8. 
AySA aims at reducing physical water losses as a way to increase water availability and improve service 
quality and extend coverage, thus delaying investments in increasing production capacity. According to its 2018 
water balance, the utility’s physical losses stood at 690,767,610 m3/year, equivalent to 36.7 percent of the year 
production of 1,884,127,810 m3. Building on actions implemented since 2007, AySA proposes to recover a total of 
123,374 m3/day through: (i) the replacement of 510 km of networks via trenchless pipe burst technology and high

areas (DMAs) (1,200 m3/day); (iv) conducting active leakage detection and repair supported by satellite detection 
technology (51,000 m3/day); and (v) the renovation of variable speed drives (VSD) in the Saavedra pumping station 
(10,000 m3/day). 
9. 
AySA’s proposal follows sound practices to identify the magnitude of key components of water losses and 
the proposed actions proved to be successful in projects already implemented by the utility and in other countries 
in the region. Pipe replacement is a reasonable approach to reduce physical losses in low-pressure systems and AySA 
will intervene the oldest pipes in its network or those most prone to breakage such as asbestos cement ones. The 
implementation of 10 DMAs including consumption meters is a continuation of previous efforts and a move to gain 
control of the system. Satellite leak detection improves the efficiency of traditional noise correlation in low-pressure 
systems, as confirmed by AySA in a pilot test. Water recovery targets from pipe replacement were set using data from 
similar previous activities and will be validated through pilots to be performed in two DMAs. Targets for satellite leak 
detection and repair were set applying typical loss rates from the burst and background estimates (BABE) model to a 
statistically probable mix of different types of breakages.  
(B) 
Energy Efficiency Improvements  
10. 
AySA is seeking to reduce energy consumption to improve its environmental and financial sustainability, 
reducing energy and maintenance costs. The total annual energy savings target is 33.93 GWh/year (92,960KWh/day), 
with active monitoring of consumption at 800 deep wells and 146 sewage pumping stations contributing to savings of 
27.14 GWh/year (through a reduction in contracted power, and balancing supply and demand), distributed self-
generation providing savings of 1.75 GWh/year (through pilot solar power plants in the San Martin and Belgrano WTPs 
and the Berazategui WWTP), and the installation of new motors with VSDs at the raw water pumping station at the 
San Martin WTP and the Saavedra water pumping station contributing 5.04 GWh/year.  
11. 
AySA has been implementing distributed energy consumption monitoring for several years now, with 
savings already achieved, and expects better results from actions contemplated under the Program, given that the 
interventions will be undertaken in larger facilities. The experience gained by AySA staff during the previous three 
stages, provides assurance that these higher savings will be achieved. The installation of solar panels has become quite 
reliable during the last decade and AySA’s estimation of 200 kW effective power out of a 1 MW facility is considered 
conservative given the plants’ geographical location. These kinds of pilots and scale-up operations are being 
successfully implemented in several LAC countries. Replacing old motors at pumping stations with new ones provided 
with VSDs is a well-known practice in the sector and AySA has already installed VSDs in other pumping stations, and is 
therefore familiar with these devices, the selection of the right equipment, and the potential results.

Allocation of Budget Across Activities and Budget Execution 
34. 
The PMOEM is prepared every five years based on works completed/service requirements/information 
from census, etc. The process for developing the next version of the PMOEM will normally start 1 to 1.5 years before 
the end of the current PMOEM. The PMOEM is prepared based on water standards or statistics and specific studies. 
For example, 1km of water and sewage connections normally requires x number of connections, y km number of pipes, 
z number of pumps with standard costs/measures for each of these elements to derive the total cost of the works. The 
PMOEM is adjusted as investments/works are further designed, as well as the impact of changes in the prices. There 
are processes in place to ensure that up to date listing of prices/costs is maintained (monthly updates, based on for 
example the prices quoted by suppliers on contracts or the results of recent studies), so that the budgeting of works is 
maintained as realistic as possible, taking into account both inflationary and exchange rate pressures. The unit 
responsible for planning and coordinating the execution of the PMOEM is adequately and competently staffed and has 
vast experience. There are policies and procedures in place to guide the preparation of the PMOEM from a technical 
perspective, including guidelines on how to cost works, monitor prices, seek explanations in case of variances greater 
than 15 percent or less than 20 percent of the expected costs/prices. An annual report is also prepared each August 
and submitted to APLA, which analyzes the execution of the PMOEM, more from a technical perspective as opposed to 
a financial perspective. AySA does, however, submit monthly reports to APLA detailing the physical and financial 
progress of each activity included in the PMOEM. 
35. 
AySA has a high degree of autonomy in budget execution within the parameters of the approved PMOEM 
and has a high institutional capacity. The Program is not protected by revenue earmarking mechanisms and, 
consequently, there is no protected share of the Program.

construction works are expected to begin as early as 2021, this Project can support job creation during the economic 
recovery period. A World Bank analysis of job generation potential of alternative infrastructure investments reveals 
that WSS projects in Argentina can support between 1,088 and 2,687 direct job-years per US$100 million invested in 
activities such as water network expansion and primary and secondary sewerage lines. Based on this estimate, the 
projects of this Program can support a total of 6,102 to 15,070 job-years, after accounting for direct and indirect jobs, 
i.e. indirect jobs are supported within the suppliers to the production process. In addition, about 75 percent of jobs in 
WSS construction require low- and semi-skilled workers, which are likely to be filled by low-income workers. To support 
the jobs agenda, AySA implements the A+T and C+T programs which seek to create new jobs through cooperatives. The 
cooperatives are associations of autonomous workers that provide the labor force for the execution of projects and 
receive compensation according to the degree of completion. The Programs also offer training and tools for workers. 
The works associated with expanding intrahousehold water connections of the Program will be implemented through 
cooperatives.

AySA’s subsidy scheme is progressive, and includes targeted subsidies, cross-subsidies and supply-side 
subsidies, as well as subsidies for network expansion. First, the National Government subsidizes capital costs, which 
are mainly investments to expand water and sanitation services to low-income areas. Subsidies for network expansion 
are highly progressive since the main beneficiaries are low-income households, which are the largest proportion of 
users without access. Second, the subsidies that cover AySA’s operating deficit benefits all users, but it is progressive 
in relative terms (the poorest 20 percent, while having only 3.6 percent of total income, receive 4.7 percent of 
subsidies). Third, the tariff structure is based on a cross-subsidy scheme, with the tariff varying according to location, 
area, quality and age of the property that is being served, which is used as a proxy for the income level of users. Finally, 
there are targeted subsidies whose beneficiaries are determined based on income and are largely progressive because 
they benefit directly low-income households.

2. 
The overall integrated fiduciary risk rating is considered Substantial. The key fiduciary risks to the

Borrower and the main implementing entity, AySA with the PforR Instrument; (ii) the risk of awarding a contract to

Planning and Budgeting 
8. 
AySA finances its operations and the PMOEM through transfers from its majority shareholder, the National 
State, by funds generated by its operations and by obtaining bank loans or issuing debt bonds in financial markets. 
Consequently, AySA depends on the transfers from the National State for the execution of the PMOEM and the

9. 
The Program will follow the Federal budget cycle and process. The formal budget process in Argentina 
consists of four stages. The first stage entails the formulation of the Executive’s budget proposal, and the second 
involves the congressional discussion of the proposal and approval of the budget law. In the third stage, the proposal 
is implemented, and in the fourth, execution of the budget is evaluated and controlled. The fiscal year runs from January 
1 to December 31. 
10. 
During the formulation stage, based on revenue estimates and forecasts of the main macroeconomic 
variables, medium-term ceilings on budget aggregates and ministry/agency expenditures are proposed by the 
National Budget Office (Oficina Nacional de Presupuesto, ONP 86) and approved by the Head of the Cabinet (based 
on established priorities and public policies). The line ministries/agencies will then prepare their preliminary draft 
budgets on this basis. In the case of the PMOEM, there is a specific unit within AySA (Coordinacion y Seguimiento de 
PMOEM) that acts as the focal point for preparation of the budget documentation relating to the PMOEM, which is 
then included in the broader AySA budget proposal to be sent to the ONP for review. Consequently, there is due process 
between the implementing agency (via the MOPW) and the ONP for negotiating the annual budget. 
11. 
The Executive must submit the annual budget87 to the National Congress88 for its approval before 
September 15, when the approval stage begins. The National Congress then evaluates the proposal through the 
Budget Committee (Comisión de Presupuesto y Hacienda) of each chamber. The budget proposal is received by the 
Chamber of Deputies, considered and dispatched, before it arrives in the Senate for analysis and approval. After the 
National Congress approves the budget, the Executive can introduce line item vetoes.

trial stage, in which case a separate judge or judicial panel conducts the trial until its completion. There is thus

ANNEX 5. SUMMARY ENVIRONMENTAL AND SOCIAL SYSTEMS ASSESSMENT 
1. 
An Environmental and Social Systems Assessment (ESSA) was carried out, according to the PforR Financing 
Policy. The ESSA examined the scope, risks, benefits and potential impacts of the Program from an environmental and 
social perspective. In addition, the ESSA examined the extent to which the legal and institutional frameworks pertinent 
to the Program as well as AySA’s corporate systems and processes are materially consistent with the core principles 
outlined in the Bank’s Policy and Directive for PforR Financing. The findings of this assessment are based on a review 
of existing information and virtual meetings with representatives of key counterparts and other relevant stakeholders. 
These discussions and the information provided allowed the team to gain detailed knowledge of social and 
environmental systems that apply to the Program. The draft ESSA was disclosed on January 5, 2021 and consulted on 
January 11, 2021 with two events held with key institutional stakeholders, cooperatives and representatives of civil 
society organizations. The draft ESSA was also available to a larger group of stakeholders for virtual comments and 
feedback. A report on the consultations, the feedback received and how i addressed is included in the final ESSA, which 
is disclosed prior to Board approval. 
2. 
The overall assessment concluded that the current systems are materially consistent with the PforR Policy 
and its core principles related to the adequacy of environmental and social regulatory and institutional systems, 
impacts on cultural and natural resources, community and workers health and safety, land management under the 
Program, cultural appropriateness and risks of potential conflict. The ESSA informed that main risks and adverse 
environmental and social impacts are sufficiently covered by the regulations and institutional capacities of AySA and 
the Regulatory Framework, although areas with an opportunity for improvement have been also identified. 
3. 
The Program will likely have overall positive environmental and social impacts on the lives and livelihoods 
of large vulnerable communities. The Program will support an increase of the offer and quality of drinking water and 
the expansion of sanitation services in targeted areas, with no significant adverse environmental impacts expected due 
to the proposed interventions. Main positive environmental effects are related to the adequate collection, treatment 
and final disposal of a greater volume of sewage, with consequent improvements in terms of soil and groundwater 
contamination; the improvement of health conditions for the new population served; and the environmental 
enhancement of the intervention areas from the sealing of septic tanks; among others. The reduction of water losses 
and energy consumption also entail environmental benefits. The Program would likely have overall positive social 
impacts on the lives and livelihoods of large vulnerable communities through the expansion of safe water and sanitation 
services, which will also improve the quality of life and reduce WASH-related health risks in targeted neighborhoods, 
and increase local employment through participation in cooperatives, thus generating income opportunities and 
promoting social inclusion. The Program will also support AySA’s citizen engagement and beneficiary feedback 
mechanisms, which will help to improve the quality of the service, and AySAs readiness for emergency response. 
4. 
Environmental Risks. The overall environmental risks and potential impacts of the Program are considered 
Substantial, basically in connection with interventions in existing sewage treatment plants given the risk and potential 
impacts associated to civil works in the construction stage (soil movements; materials transportation and handling; 
noise, dust, debris generation; among others) and, once treatment capacity is expanded, to the increased volume of 
treated effluents to be discharged to water bodies during the operation stage. However, these proposed works are of 
a limited scale, will increase the level of treatment of effluents, and will take place within AySA's premises. Also, they 
will not involve new discharge pipelines or discharge points, which means that the risks and potential environmental 
impacts to the water bodies where the treated effluents are discharged have been taken into account and, accordingly, 
evaluated in the original Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs) of the wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs). The 
original EIAs considered the modular design of the plants (i.e. to be built in stages) and the maximum volume of 
effluents once all modules are constructed and fully operational. The original EIAs will be updated and presented to the 
environmental authorities as part of the renewal of the environmental licenses. The main specific risks that would need

ANNEX 8. ADJUSTMENTS TO THE COUNTRY PROGRAM IN RESPONSE TO COVID-19 
1. 
This annex presents adjustments made to the World Bank Group’s (WBG’s) program in response to the 
COVID-19 crisis endorsed by the government of Argentina. They follow the four pillars of the WBG’s COVID 19 Crisis 
Response Approach Paper: (i) emergency support to health interventions for saving lives; (ii) social response for 
protecting poor and vulnerable people; (iii) economic response for saving livelihoods, preserving jobs, and ensuring 
more sustainable business growth and job creation; and (iv) support for strengthening policies, institutions and 
investments for sustainable recovery. 
Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the country and government response 
2. 
Despite its adoption of strict quarantine measures early on, Argentina ranks among the top ten countries 
with COVID-19 cases.  Although the GoA moved swiftly in imposing a mandatory quarantine to contain the COVID 
spread, following more than six months of varying degrees of lockdown in the Buenos Aires City and Province, 
confinement measures were recently relaxed despite rapid spread, especially in the interior. With almost 1.9 million 
reported cases of infections and over 45,000 deaths, Argentina now posts one of the highest contagion rates in the 
world, and COVID cases are spreading to other provinces which have limited hospital capacity.  
3. 
The economic crisis, worsened by the lockdown, is having deep impacts on employment, incomes and 
poverty. Argentina’s economy was already in a fragile situation when the pandemic hit, entering into its third year of 
economic contraction, facing high unemployment and more than a third of workers in the informal sector. The 
lockdown led to a collapse in domestic demand and resulted in a rapid decline in employment and increase in poverty 
rates. Based on official statistics employment dropped from 44.2 to 33.4 percent between the first and second quarters 
of 2020, and partially recovered to 37.4 in the third quarter, as lockdown measures were gradually eased. Following a 
similar trend, the share of active population in the labor market declined from 47.1 to 38.4 percent and then increased 
to 42.3 percent in the same period, while unemployment climbed from 10.4 to 13.1 percent in the second quarter, 
declining to 11.7 percent in the third quarter. This occurred within the context of many firm closures105, offsetting the 
effects of the emergency package that temporarily banned termination of employment and offered payroll subsidies 
and cheap credit to the private sector. In addition to expanding the coverage of existing social protection schemes, 
emergency transfer programs have been launched to reduce impacts on the most vulnerable groups and have been 
phased out by end-2020. 
4. 
Although the economy is slowly recovering as lockdown measures have been lifted and idle production 
capacity is restarted, the recovery pace is expected to be sluggish. High uncertainty owing to the pandemic and 
macroeconomic imbalances, price and capital controls, difficulties in accessing credit and softened consumer demand 
will hold back new investments while the need for fiscal consolidation and lack of access to international capital 
markets will limit the scope for demand stimulus. After a projected contraction of 10.6 percent in real GDP in 2020 
following two years of recession in 2018 and 2019, the rebound in 2021 is only projected at 4.9 percent.106 The 
economy will remain below its potential output and not reach 2019 GDP-levels (already a low base) before 2023.

6. 
Support to health for saving lives. The Bank provided Argentina with $35m COVID emergency support as 
part of the first Batch of the Global MPA Program as well as support through Bank-facilitated procurement. The project 
focuses on strengthening health systems to cope with the pandemic and financing necessary medical supplies and 
equipment. The Electricity Emergency Response Social Tariff Transition in the Province of Buenos Aires Project is being 
restructured to include an emergency response for food program to the poorest. Similarly, the Argentina Universal 
Health Coverage Program has just been restructured to expedite disbursements and provide more liquidity to the 
system; an Additional Financing of $250m is being prepared to cover financing gaps. The government has also 
requested $300m in Bank financing for its maternity and early childhood program, “Plan 1000 Dias.”  
7. 
Social response for protecting poor and vulnerable people. To expand protection of the poor from the 
worsening crisis, the Bank team prepared a $300m Additional Financing for the Universal Childhood Assistance Project  
to expand the reach of social protection of vulnerable and unemployed families with children which was approved by 
the Board in March 2019. The team has processed a $44m restructuring and extension of the Plan Belgrano Water 
Supply and Sanitation Project to bring safe water to vulnerable Wichi indigenous communities in the north, an area 
affected by a Dengue epidemic followed by the COVID-19 pandemic. The present $300m Program for Results will 
expand water, sanitation and hygiene in vulnerable urban and peri-urban communities. Additional financing to expand 
health and social protection services for the poor is being considered. 
8. 
Economic response for saving livelihoods, preserving jobs, and ensuring more sustainable business 
growth and job creation. For FY21 the Bank is preparing a $320 million urban railway project in the Buenos Aires 
Metropolitan Area to provide access to jobs in the COVID-19 Recovery Phase, with strong focus on hygiene and viability 
of urban transit systems. A Level 1 Restructuring of the Salado Integrated River Basin Management Project is being 
concluded to provide access to improved housing and local jobs in vulnerable communities in this metropolitan area. 
A joint Poverty and Infrastructure team is assessing potential job impacts—direct and indirect—from different forms 
of investment to help inform the government about the relative potential of those urgent public investments to 
stimulate demand.  
9. 
Support for strengthening policies, institutions and investments for resilient and sustainable recovery. 
The Bank will continue reviewing current public expenditure systems and support improvements through 
automatization and digital management systems, focusing on improving transparency, efficiency and targeting of social 
programs. For FY21, the Bank is also preparing an $80m Additional Financing for the Public Sector Modernization 
Project with a focus on digital solutions for health and social protection systems and to ensure business continuity at 
times of crisis. This project will have an important component to support digital inclusion of remote and less connected 
regions.
