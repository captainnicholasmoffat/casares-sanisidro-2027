 Overview 
 WHO estimates that over one billion young people 
globally are at risk of hearing loss due to sound 
exposure in recreational settings. The risk of 
permanent hearing injury due to unsafe listening is both avoidable, and costly. 
 In the face of this growing threat, governments, public-health 
agencies, those involved in the creation, distribution and 
amplification of music, the private sector, civil society, and other 
stakeholders, all have a duty of care in understanding the sound 
levels to which audiences and consumers are being exposed, and 
creating environments that facilitate safe listening behaviours. 
 To address this, WHO, as part of their Make Listening Safe initiative, 
developed the Global standard for safe listening venues and events 
which provides a common understanding of 
safe listening in entertainment venues and events. The Standard 
comprises six “features” which, when implemented, allow audience 
members around the world to enjoy amplified music with protection 
of their hearing, while also preserving the integrity of the artistic 
experience.
