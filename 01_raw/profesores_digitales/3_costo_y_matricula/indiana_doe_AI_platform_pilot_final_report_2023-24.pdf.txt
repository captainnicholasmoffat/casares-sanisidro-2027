Introduction
Grant Background
In August 2023, the Indiana Department of Education (IDOE) invited accredited school
corporations and school organizations to apply for a one-time competitive grant opportunity to
fund a pilot of an artificial intelligence (AI) powered platform of their choosing for the 2023-2024
school year. This opportunity supported a one-year implementation and funded subscription
fees and professional development to support high-dosage tutoring for students and reduce
teacher workload through the use of an AI platform. This grant provided an opportunity for
school corporations to target support to a specific building, grade level, subject area, or student
population by integrating an AI platform with a cohort of teachers and students. Schools were
encouraged to focus on student needs in response to academic impact data.
The awardees consisted of 112 schools. This included 2,466 teachers1 and 45,244 students
from 36 school corporations across the state.
AI Platforms
IDOE established AI platform vendor requirements for schools to use as part of their selection
process. In alignment with local control structures, schools had the autonomy to choose vendors
that best met their unique use cases and establish a contractual relationship directly with their
chosen vendor. The following platforms were selected by schools and met the platform vendor
requirements: Amira, Chat for Schools (Skill Struck), Edia, Khanmigo (Khan Academy), and
SchoolJoy. Table 1 shows the number of school corporations, individual schools, teachers, and
students utilizing each platform as part of the AI pilot.2

Teacher Feedback Survey
In April 2024, IDOE launched a survey to collect feedback from all teachers who were part of
the pilot grant. The survey allowed teachers to provide direct input on their experience using the
AI platforms. The primary goals of the survey were to understand platform usefulness, student
and teacher impact, training effectiveness, and the overall experience using the platform. The
survey collected both qualitative and quantitative data, with qualitative data being analyzed
through an open-coding process. Preliminary survey results and site visit observations were
presented at the Indiana State Board of Education meeting in June 2024.

Teacher Impact
One of the primary aims of the survey was to understand the pilot’s impact on teaching
practices. In a Likert scale question, 53% rated the overall impact of using this platform on their
teaching practice as positive or very positive, with nearly 40% indicating unchanged, as shown
in Figure 3.
Figure 3. Impact on teaching practice.
As a follow-up to the Likert scale question, respondents were asked to explain their rating in an
open-ended question. The top responses4 are outlined in the following sections with
exemplifying quotes.
Saving Time
The most frequent response related to the impact on teaching practices was the ability for
teachers to save time using the AI platform. The remaining top responses were all closely tied to
saving time by using the platforms.
The responses included examples of how time saved from using the AI platform could be
allocated to more instructional time and providing support for students.

Data and Assessment
Teachers reported the ways the AI platforms could be used to assess students and provide data
that could then be used to help teachers provide targeted support.
Communication
Using the platforms for communication purposes was another frequent response. This included
teacher use of the platform to assist with tailoring communication to sound more professional or
positive, generating recommendation letters in less time, and improving communication with
parents.
Extra Practice for Students
Teachers reported that extra practice generated by the platform helped students reinforce topics
and lessons both in the classroom and at home. Teachers shared that students could select the
areas where they needed additional practice, or the platforms provided an avenue for
assistance when they were stuck on a particular topic. While this is student focused, lesson and
skill reinforcement helps teachers progress through curriculum, and this can save time for
teachers.

Student Impact
Another primary goal of the survey was to understand the pilot’s impact on student learning. In a
Likert scale question, 53% of teachers rated the overall impact of using this platform on their
students’ learning as positive or very positive, with nearly 40% indicating unchanged as shown
in Figure 4.
Figure 4. Impact on student learning.
As a follow-up to the Likert scale question, respondents were asked to explain their rating in an
open-ended question. The top responses are outlined in the following sections with exemplifying
quotes.
Tutoring, Guidance, and Feedback
Teachers discussed the ability of the platforms to tutor students by guiding them through a
question or problem rather than providing a direct answer. Many of the platforms are designed

help.

Implementation Lessons Learned
The benefit of launching the grant pilot included identifying the strengths and challenges of this new
grant program to better understand the impact. While many challenges were related to the platforms
as described in previous sections, implementation lessons learned can be observed.
Usage
Actual usage was lower than what schools requested, and some teacher responses reflected this.
Factors that impacted this include the accelerated launch of the grant and a delay for some schools in
completing vendor training. Some teachers indicated they did not use the platform enough or had little
time to gain familiarity. This can be addressed by ensuring grant processes provide ample time for
schools to adequately prepare, schedule training, and implement platforms with fidelity whenever
feasible.
Training
20% of teacher responses said they did not attend any training sessions provided by the vendor. Due
to this question being multiple choice, no claims on reasons can be made. This challenge will be
addressed through verification documentation and regular check-ins with the schools to assist with
any potential barriers in training or general implementation.
Additional Guidance
As discussed in the Challenges section, some teachers indicated there was not as much relevant
content for their subject area. Schools may need further guidance beyond the vendor requirements on
what to evaluate and consider when selecting a vendor, including subject areas. The other two
identified challenges - user experience and microphone issues - might also benefit from additional
guidance. While IDOE cannot change platform capabilities or address potential technical challenges,
guidance on what to do if teachers encounter these types of issues may be necessary, as well as the
option for IDOE to facilitate feedback to the platform vendors on behalf of schools.
Conclusion
Indiana’s AI-Powered Platform Pilot Grant provided an opportunity for 112 schools across the
state to utilize an AI platform for high-dosage tutoring for students, reducing teacher workload.
Indiana recognized the importance of gathering direct feedback on the pilot from those who can
best speak to the experience of implementation - teachers. The teacher feedback survey
highlighted both the impacts and challenges of using the AI platforms for teaching practices and
student learning. Teachers reported positive impacts on their teaching practices including saving
time, being able to generate content and ideas, access to data, communication assistance, and
more. Some of the teacher-reported positive impacts on student learning included access to
tutoring and guidance, direct feedback on work, enjoyment for students using the platforms,
ability to obtain extra practice and support, academic improvement, increased independence
and confidence, and more. While the pilot has concluded, efforts to support the use of AI and
the growth of AI literacy are ongoing through multiple avenues including the Digital Learning
