Summary of Key Findings 
●​ Educators rating their attitudes about AI as positive or very positive increased from 25% 
before the grant to 69% at the end of the grant. 
●​ Educators rating their attitudes about AI as negative or very negative decreased from 
28% before the grant to 9% at the end of the grant. 
●​ 72% of teachers who reported using grant-funded AI platforms rated the overall impact 
on their teaching practice as moderate, significant, or very significant. 
●​ The most commonly mentioned impacts on teaching practices were the time-saving 
benefits of using the AI platform; the ability to generate content and ideas; and support 
related to data, feedback, and assessment. 
●​ 66% of teachers who reported using grant-funded AI platforms rated the overall impact 
on their students’ learning as positive or very positive. 
●​ The most commonly mentioned impacts on student learning were those related to 
tutoring and feedback; improved educational content; and the ability to provide 
personalized, differentiated learning experiences. 
 
Introduction 
Grant Background 
Indiana’s annual Digital Learning Grant is designed to support public school corporations and 
charter schools in their efforts to: 
●​ Leverage technology to enhance student learning experiences. 
●​ Foster innovation in educational practices. 
●​ Promote effective digital pedagogies for teachers. 
This grant is part of a broader initiative by the Indiana Department of Education (IDOE) to 
integrate technology meaningfully into classrooms, ensuring that both students and educators 
are equipped for success in blended and virtual learning environments. 
The 2024-25 Digital Learning Grant prioritized initiatives that sought to fund projects 
emphasizing safe and secure access to artificial intelligence (AI) platforms utilizing large 
language models to support student tutoring and teacher administrative tasks.  
Of the 99 grant awardees, 53 school corporations funded AI projects. This report focuses on 
those recipients. This included 8,941 educators who supported 97,146 students. 
Previously, IDOE offered the AI-Powered Platform Pilot Grant during the 2023-24 school year. 
At the conclusion of that grant, a survey was conducted among participating teachers. These 
results were collected and published in the AI-Powered Platform Pilot Grant Final Report.

AI Platforms 
IDOE established AI platform vendor requirements for schools to use as part of their selection 
process. In alignment with local control structures, schools had the autonomy to choose vendors 
that best met the unique needs of their community and establish a contractual relationship 
directly with their chosen vendor. The following platforms were selected by schools and met the 
platform vendor requirements: Amira, Chat for Schools (Skill Struck), Edia, Khanmigo (Khan 
Academy), MagicSchool, SchoolAI, and Snorkl. Table 1 shows the number of school 
corporations, teachers, and students utilizing each platform as part of the AI pilot.   
Table 1. Breakdown of grant participation by platform vendor.

This report features responses from teachers participating in AI initiatives funded by the Digital 
Learning Grant during the 2024-25 school year. The primary goals of this survey are to 
understand platform usefulness, student and teacher impact, training effectiveness, and the 
overall experience using the platform.

Attitudes Towards AI 
Educators were asked to indicate their attitudes towards AI before and after using it as part of 
the grant. In a Likert scale question, 25% of educators rated their attitudes towards AI before the 
grant as positive or very positive. By the end of the grant cycle, 69% rated their attitudes 
towards AI as positive or very positive. Educators rating their attitudes as negative or very 
negative decreased from 28% before the grant to 9% at the end of the grant.

Content and Idea Generation 
Educator responses highlighted that the ability to generate instructional content and ideas using 
the AI platform improved their practice. Teachers mentioned leveraging AI for creation in various 
ways, including developing lesson plans, rubrics, depth-of-knowledge questions, and curriculum 
maps.  
 
Data, Feedback, and Assessment  
Educators shared that AI tools were useful for assessing students and producing insights that 
helped them develop future instructional support. Educators indicated that AI platforms assisted 
them in providing timely, actionable feedback to students. Some educators stated that platforms 
helped them support student growth and improvement.

Student Impact 
Another key objective of the survey was to evaluate the grant activities' impact on student 
learning. In a Likert scale question, 66% of teachers who reported using the platform rated the 
overall impact on their students’ learning as positive or very positive, as shown in Figure 6. This

increase in the percentage of teachers rating the impact as very positive.

Tutoring and Feedback 
 
Teachers discussed the value of the platform’s ability to tutor and guide students to improve 
their work and help them reach answers to questions without providing those answers outright. 
They stated that students were able to get immediate feedback, especially in the area of writing, 
which resulted in improved student work. AI tutors were a source students could use when the 
teacher was not available or when the students were at home. Students were able to use the 
platforms to study for upcoming assessments.  
 
 
 
Engagement and Enjoyment 
 
Teachers indicated that students enjoyed their work on the AI platforms. They reported 
increased engagement and higher motivation when students used the platform. While some 
indicated that students did not like it, more reported that students enjoyed using the platforms.

Differentiation and Personalized Learning 
 
Teachers stated that differentiated materials positively impacted student learning, providing 
personalized learning experiences for their students. Materials were able to be adjusted to 
match the instructional needs of individual students. Teachers commented that this helped them 
support students with disabilities, students learning English, and high-ability students. 
 
 
 
Academic Improvement 
 
Some educators observed academic growth and improvement among students who engaged 
with the platforms. However, as with all survey responses, these observations are self-reported 
and should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of student outcomes.

Training

was an IDOE requirement for any platform vendor that schools selected. Vendor training was required 
to consider thoughtful integration of the platform within blended and/or virtual learning models,

on a schedule agreed upon between the vendor and school, and guidance to support teachers in

Challenges 
As with any program or platform, teachers and students encountered some challenges. The 
challenges outlined in this section were identified in responses to multiple open-ended questions 
and are not in ranked order. 
Overreliance 
Some educators expressed concern about the potential for both teachers and students to become 
overreliant on AI and the potential consequences associated with this.  
 
 
User Experience 
Several users expressed that both students and teachers faced obstacles when interacting with the 
platforms. Some reported that the platform was difficult for students to navigate or didn’t always 
function properly. 
 
 
Content for All Subject Areas 
Some teachers stated that they believed AI platforms were not applicable to their subject areas or 
grade level.

Next Steps 
IDOE continues to provide additional guidance and support for educators across the state of Indiana. 
The results of this report may contribute to future projects related to artificial intelligence. 
●​ Data shows educators who participated in the implementation of AI platforms showed more 
positive attitudes toward AI. IDOE continues its efforts to support similar projects through the 
Digital Learning Grant taking place during the current school year, 2025-26, and its plan for 
the next grant iteration during the 2026-27 school year. 
●​ Data shows 64% of educators reported attending a training session. IDOE will take steps to 
increase this number, including connecting with current grantees, requiring a more specific 
commitment from future grantees, and connecting with vendors about training opportunities. 
●​ Data shows educators identify time savings as the most significant benefit to their teaching 
practice. While productivity is a meaningful benefit of AI, the potential for teachers to 
differentiate content, personalize learning, and make data-driven decisions holds great value. 
IDOE remains committed to highlighting real examples of educators using AI to innovate in 
their classrooms. 
●​ Data shows educators report that AI’s support for student tutoring and feedback provides the 
largest impact on student learning. IDOE plans to amplify student voices who can speak to 
their experiences with AI in classrooms.  
 
Conclusion 
The Digital Learning Grant funded 99 projects across the state of Indiana, including 53 that funded 
the implementation of an AI-powered educational platform. To provide transparency and inform 
future work, a survey was sent to all participating educators, and this report analyzes 2,096 
responses. The teacher feedback survey highlighted platform usage, attitudes toward AI, and the 
impacts and challenges of using the AI platforms for teaching practices and student learning. Data 
showed that teachers who gained experience working with AI platforms showed much more positive 
attitudes about AI. They reported using AI to save time, generate content and ideas, provide 
guidance and feedback for students, differentiate and personalize learning experiences, improve 
communication, and engage students.
