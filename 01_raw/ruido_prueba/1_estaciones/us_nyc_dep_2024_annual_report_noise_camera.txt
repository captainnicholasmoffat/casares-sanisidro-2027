Annual Report for Noise Camera Enforcement Program  
Pursuant to Local Law 7 of 2024 
January 31, 2025 
Bureau of Environmental Compliance, NYC Department of Environmental Protection 
 
Background 
Noise emissions from loud vehicle engines or mufflers can impinge on the quality of life in New 
York City. Some vehicle noise is so loud that it can even be a source of harmful public health 
impacts, such as sleep deprivation and hearing loss. In recent years, complaints about loud vehicle 
engines and mufflers have increased. The Department of Environmental Protection’s Noise 
Camera Enforcement Program was created to combat this excessive noise. With strategically 
placed noise cameras, the Department (“DEP”) identifies vehicles that exceed the New York City 
Noise Code’s limits. A summons can be issued to any vehicle found to be violating these limits 
using evidence gathered by the camera. 
Local Law 7 
Local Law 7 of 2024 mandates a noise camera program that detects motor vehicles exceeding 
noise limits under the Noise Code. Subject to appropriations, DEP must install no fewer than 5 
noise cameras in each borough by September 2025. DEP currently has nine cameras but has not 
received appropriations to expand the program to that extent.   
Overview of the Program 
The noise cameras are comprised of an array of microphones and cameras, including a “fisheye” 
(panoramic) and license plate reader (LPR) camera. When the microphones detect that a certain 
noise threshold is exceeded, the fisheye camera pinpoints the source of noise by placing a red dot 
on the source.  Using the photo of the license plate that is captured by the LPR camera, the owner 
of the vehicle can be identified. Then, a summons for engine/muffler noise can be issued by DEP 
staff. Summonses for engine and muffler noise are issued pursuant to section 24-236(e) of the 
Noise Code. However, DEP not only uses the noise cameras to address engine/muffler noise but 
also uses them to address horn honking under section 24-237(a) and music from vehicles under 
section 24-233(b). The summonses are returnable at the NYC Office of Administrative Trials and 
Hearings (OATH.) If found in violation at OATH, civil penalties are imposed against the vehicle 
owner.

Totals (all locations) 
During the period from June 2021 through December 31, 2024, the total number of summonses 
for all locations was 1,602. However, up until August 2023, DEP only had one camera.   
During the period from June 2021 through December 31, 2024, the number of summonses issued 
on a daily basis was fewer than one. During that period, the number of summonses issued on a 
weekly basis was 2.15. During that period, the total number of summonses issued on a monthly 
basis was 9.4.
