1. Introduction 
As a Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA), it is the responsibility of Wrexham County Borough Council (WCBC) 
to manage the local flood risk from surface water, groundwater and ordinary watercourses such as streams 
and ditches (including lakes and ponds). The following report has been prepared in accordance with the 
requirements of the Section 19 of the Flood and Water Management Act 2010 (FWMA) and WCBCs Local 
Flood Risk Management Strategy (LFRMS) and Plan (LFRMP). 
This investigation has been carried out following recent internal flooding of residential properties. 
In accordance with the requirements of Section 19 of the FWMA (2010): 
1) On becoming aware of a flood in its area, a lead local flood authority must, to the extent that it 
considers it necessary or appropriate, investigate- 
a) which risk management authorities had relevant flood risk management functions; and 
b) whether each of those risk management authorities had exercised or was proposing to exercise 
those functions in response to the flood.  
2) where an authority carries out an investigation under subsection (1) it must- 
a) publish the results of its investigation, and 
b) notify any relevant risk management authorities. 
2. Flood Chronology  
2.1 Recent Events 
On Wednesday 16th October 2024, a band of high intensity rainfall travelled northwards across the east of 
the county borough. This stalled over the area dropping approximately 63.2 mm of rainfall across the Wych 
Brook and Emral Brook upstream of Worthenbury over a period of ten hours (Met Office, 2025). This 
caused the water level in Worthenbury Brook to rise to 3.422 m before the data logger failed from 
inundation. The failure occurred because the logger is designed to monitor low water levels for abstraction 
downstream. However, given that the flood embankment was exceeded in locations where the height of 
the flood embankment had settled, it is estimated the flood peak could have been as high as 14.0 mAOD.  
An operational response was instigated at 13:45 allowing for NRW officers to attend Worthenbury from 
15:00 on the afternoon of 16th October. At approximately 21:00 it was reported to Natural Resources Wales 
that the flood embankment defense protecting The Cobbles, Quinton, The Manor and Manor Cottage was 
overtopping into the defended area on the southern, landward side of the defense. The latter three of 
these properties form separate parts of a Grade II Listed Building. By the time that the overtopping had 
been observed, water ingression under the flood embankment had also been noted by residents and 
officers from NRW in an area to the south east of the defended area. It is understood by the LLFA that this 
problem was existing from a high flow event in September 2024, and NRW had mitigated this through the 
placing and securing of a visqueen cover until a more permanent repair could be made.  In addition, 
photographic evidence provided by NRW shows flood water overtopping on to the public highway which 
then found its way into the driveways of the properties.  
All four properties within the defended area subsequently started to flood around 22:00 and had reportedly 
reached a height of 0.91 m inside one of the properties by 03:00 on 17th October 2025. The flood water had 
to be pumped into the river over the next three days following the event.  
2.2 Flood History

The village of Worthenbury suffers frequent flooding from Emral, Wych and Worthenbury Brook due to the 
large upstream catchment area. The proximity of the village to the confluence of Worthenbury Brook and 
the River Dee also means that water levels are frequently raised immediately downstream of the village. It 
is very common for the village to become cut off following flood events as flood waters often block the 
B5069.  
The flood problem surrounding the properties covered in this report are highway surface water runoff, 
surcharged land drains and ordinary watercourses. During larger floods there is major inundation of the 
B5069 into the village, such as occurred in October and November 2000. The flooding of these properties 
has been documented for the past thirty years. The property known as The Cobbles has been recorded as 
suffering from flooding in February 1990, March 1991, February 1994, March 1995, winters 1998 and 1999. 
The properties known as Quinton, The Manor and Manor Cottage having their access cut off during these 
winters as well. During the floods of October and November 2000 all four properties suffered twice from 
major flooding. 
The above properties have been protected by a horseshoe shaped flood defense which was constructed in 
2004. However, it should be noted that The Cobbles regularly suffers from groundwater flooding issues. 
Bow waves from the flooded highway also contribute to the flooding of this property.   
3. Current Management Regime 
The flood defenses which protect the houses that were most impacted by flooding are maintained by 
Natural Resources Wales (NRW) who are the risk management authority for main river flooding. A range of 
landowners have riparian ownership over both the river and an ordinary watercourse which interacts with 
the floodplain. Wrexham County Borough Council are responsible for the highway and the culverts which 
runs underneath it.

4. Flood Event Details 
Date of Flood Event: 16th October 2024 
Date Reported to WCBC: 17th October 2024 
Date of Investigation: 18th October 2024 
Reason for investigation: Internal flooding of 4 residential properties, extensive flooding of residential 
gardens and flooding of B5069.  
Location: The area to the south of the village of Worthenbury, namely the properties known as Quinton, 
The Manor, Manor Cottage and The Cobbles. 
Event Duration: approximately 9 hours 
Flood Duration: approximately 2 days 
Identified Source: The flood waters were noted to be from 4 main sources: 
• 
Wych Brook 
• 
Emral Brook 
• 
Worthenbury Brook 
• 
The unnamed ordinary watercourse running alongside the B5069.

4.2 Cause / Pathway: 
Figure 5: A schematic showing the direction of flooding where blue shows the flooding from main river sources and 
the green shows the flooding from the ordinary watercourse.  
In the initial stages of the flood event, the flood defence is known to have behaved in a typical manner. The 
flood waters from Wych and Emral Brook left the channel and curved around into the field. In addition to 
this, pluvial flooding would have occurred in the field from the unnamed ordinary watercourse approaching 
the defended area from the south west. It is noted that this ordinary watercourse is higher topographically 
than the highway near to the affected properties. The combination of the multiple flooding sources likely 
created locally elevated water levels on the south western side of the defence. This resulted in overtopping 
of the flood embankment in two places: one onto the public and one into the defended area.  
It is widely corroborated by the residents that the B5069 flooded first. During the first few hours of the 
flood event, flooding on the highway can be attributed to two sources. The first of these is from the 
ordinary watercourse exceeding the channel and flooding the highway from the east. This was exacerbated 
by the second source of flooding which was the overtopping of the flood embankment. The water on the 
highway was unable to drain through the gullies and into the culvert that runs within the property 
boundary of The Cobbles. This was because the flap valve at the outfall of system had been sealed in its 
closed position by rising water levels in Worthenbury Brook causing flooding to the field in the north. It is 
unclear if water backed up through the septic tank systems within the defended area at this time, nor if a 
lack of silt within this system would have had an impact on the flood event. The flood water rose to a level 
which overtopped the kerbs on the highway. Flood water then entered the defended area through the 
grounds of The Cobbles and under the wooden fence marking the boundary of the affected properties.  
At around 21:00, it was reported that floodwater was entering the embankment under a recent NRW 
repair. The likely cause of this has been identified as seepage through the root system of a felled tree which 
was removed.

Further anecdotes from the residents suggest that the floodwater began overtopping the flood 
embankment to the rear of the properties around 22:00. Given the bowl-like topography inside the flood 
embankment, this water, combined with that from the highway, pooled and was unable to exit the defence 
until pumping was commenced by a local farmer.  
It is noted that the screen protecting the outlet in the field to the south accumulated a build up debris 
which was evident when the floodwater receded. It cannot be determined if this a result of suspended 
debris building up on the screen as the flood level decreased or if it built up during the flood event. 
Testimony from NRW suggests that this screen was clear before the water level rose in the late afternoon.  
In addition to the above, the flap valve in the ownership of WCBC Highways was noted to be heavily 
vegetated following the flood event. The flap valve is believed to have functioned as designed despite this 
because the vegetation did not prevent the closure of the valve as the flood water rose in Worthenbury 
Brook.

During the construction of the flood embankment, the ordinary watercourse was maintained through the 
overflow system installed by Environment Agency Wales who later passed the responsibility of the system 
to NRW. There is no indication that the ordinary watercourse still flows through the culverted section 
within the land of The Cobbles and The Manor. This culvert continues to convey surface water from the 
highway and the discharge from septic tanks. Given the latter, it remains in private ownership until the 
culvert crosses under the public highway.  
A full and complete survey was unviable due to the amount of silt and water held in the private section of 
the culvert. As a result, WCBC have been unable to determine the location of the septic tank connections 
and have relied on information from the residents in the above assumptions.  
4.4.2 Catchment Hydrology 
Testimony received by both WCBC and NRW suggests that the Worthenbury Brook catchment is becoming 
flashier over time. This means that the river rises rapidly before receding quickly. WCBC have requested 
information from other Risk Management Authorities (RMAs) outside the scope of this report, but 
responses have suggested that no significant changes have occurred in the catchment.  
5. Conclusions 
In conclusion, the flood event of Wednesday 16th October 2024 was the result of high intensity rainfall over

a number of hours. The levels of water reached such a depth as to cause overtopping of the flood 
embankment into the defended area and on to the public highway. This exacerbated flooding on the 
highway, as outfalls where compromised. As a result, flood water entered the defended area to both the 
north west and the south east. This then caused significant flooding to four properties which lasted for 
approximately two days. The flood water had to be removed from the defended area using a pump which 
was provided by a local landowner.
