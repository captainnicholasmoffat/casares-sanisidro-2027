Virginia Speed Camera Statute
• Speed cameras can be used to issue citations for speeding at 
least 10 mph over the speed limit in school zones, work 
zones, and high-risk intersection segments.
• A violation is punishable by up to a $100 civil penalty.
• Vendors typically mail citations and collect payments on 
behalf of law enforcement agencies.
Virginia State Crime Commission

=====PAGE 4=====
Virginia Speed Camera Statute
• Requires notice and an opportunity to contest a citation in 
court; however, the elements of the offense and burden of 
proof are different than a traffic infraction.
• A traffic infraction penalizes the driver of the vehicle and 
requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt.
Virginia State Crime Commission
4

=====PAGE 5=====
Virginia Speed Camera Statute
• Presumes that the owner, lessee, or renter of a vehicle was 
the driver; however, this presumption is rebuttable.
The statute does not specify the burden of proof.
−A civil matter is generally by a preponderance of the evidence.
−An argument could be made that the statute requires proof 
beyond a reasonable doubt.
• Speed camera violations are not considered a conviction or 
made part of a driver’s record, and cannot be used for 
insurance purposes.

Scenario #1
• Lucy’s vehicle is observed driving 37 mph in a 25 mph 
school zone.
Stopped by a law enforcement officer and issued a summons. 
Summons transmitted to the general district court and publicly 
available on the OES court website.
Summons can be prepaid or Lucy can appear in court.
If prepaid or convicted, Lucy will have to pay a fine plus court costs.
A prepayment or conviction is transmitted to the DMV and points are 
assessed on Lucy’s driver’s record, which can be considered by 
insurance companies and private employers.

Scenario #2
• Sally’s vehicle is detected by a speed camera driving 37 mph 
in a 25 mph school zone.
Sally is the only registered owner and is issued a civil citation.
Sally pays the $100 citation directly to the vendor.
Information about the citation is not made publicly available or

Scenario #3
• Linus’ vehicle is detected by a speed camera driving 37 mph 
in a 25 mph school zone.
Linus is the only registered owner and is issued a civil citation.
Linus had loaned his vehicle to Schroeder.
Linus can:
−Pay the $100 citation directly to the vendor;
−File an affidavit with the court stating that he was not the driver; or,
−Testify in court that he was not the driver.
If Linus contests the citation in court, it will be available online and 
court costs and fees will be assessed if he is found guilty.

Scenario #4

KEY FINDINGS

=====PAGE 11=====
Key Findings
• Virginia enacted legislation in 2020 to allow speed camera use in 
school zones and work zones (Va. Code § 46.2-882.1).
• As of January 2025, 37 law enforcement agencies reported use in 
school zones and 4 agencies reported use in work zones.
290 devices in school zones; and,
8 devices in work zones.
• The statute was amended in 2024 to authorize use at high-risk 
intersection segments.
No high-risk intersection segment use was reported as of January 2025.
Virginia State Crime Commission
11

=====PAGE 12=====
Key Findings
• The number of law enforcement agencies using speed cameras 
has increased significantly:
2022: 5 agencies (92,062 citations issued)
2023: 13 agencies (326,614 citations issued)
2024: 39 law enforcement agencies (597,085 citations issued)
• An additional 16 law enforcement agencies received speed 
camera equipment during 2025.
Agencies must report use to VSP by January 15th each year.
Virginia State Crime Commission
12

=====PAGE 13=====
Key Findings
• Almost $60 million in civil penalties has been collected from 
speed camera citations:
2022: $4.2 million
2023: $19.9 million
2024: $33.7 million
• Currently, only local law enforcement agencies are using speed 
cameras; therefore, revenue is only being paid to localities.
No restrictions on how revenue is spent.
No requirement to report how revenue is spent.
Virginia State Crime Commission

Key Findings
• Research shows that speed cameras can be effective for 
reducing speed, crashes, and changing driver behavior.
• The impact of speed cameras on highway safety 
outcomes in Virginia cannot be fully determined due to 
their recent implementation.

=====PAGE 22=====
Court Proceedings
• Uncertainty exists over whether all speed camera citations must 
be prosecuted through the court system.
• Prosecuting all citations in court will result in:
Resource needs;
Case information being viewable online; and,
Court costs to the defendant.
• Virginia’s statute could be amended to clarify that only contested 
citations and cases of nonpayment proceed through the court. 
Virginia State Crime Commission
22

=====PAGE 23=====
Uniform Citations
• Virginia’s statute does not require a uniform speed camera 
citation.
Citation formats vary across localities based on the use of 
different vendors, as well as locality preferences.
• At least 4 states require a uniform citation.
• One state requires an in-state payment and return address on 
the citation.
Virginia State Crime Commission
23

=====PAGE 24=====
Warning Periods
• Virginia’s statute does not require a warning period before 
issuing speed camera citations.
Many localities in Virginia implemented a 30 day warning period 
after placing a speed camera.
• Some states statutorily require warning periods, such as:
Warning period for the first 30 days;
First violation is always a warning; and,
Warning notice that resets after a period of time.
Virginia State Crime Commission
24

=====PAGE 25=====
Civil Penalty Structure
• Virginia’s statute allows for up to a $100 civil penalty per 
violation, therefore localities can determine how much to assess.
• Other states have adopted various penalty structures:
Flat penalty;
Maximum penalty;
Escalating penalty based on speed;
Escalating penalty based on number of prior violations;
Traffic safety course or community service; or,
Reduced penalty for lower-income individuals.
Virginia State Crime Commission
25

=====PAGE 26=====
Revenue Sharing
• Under Virginia’s statute, revenue from citations in school zones 
and work zones issued by local law enforcement is paid to 
localities.
• Revenue for citations in school zones and work zones issued by 
Virginia State Police (VSP) is paid to the Literary Fund.
• Revenue for citations in high-risk intersection segments is paid 
into the Virginia Highway Safety Improvement Program.
• Virginia could reconsider how this revenue is apportioned.
Virginia State Crime Commission
26

=====PAGE 27=====
Revenue Limits
• Virginia’s statute does not limit the amount of revenue that a locality 
can collect from speed camera citations.
• In Maryland, speed camera revenue over 10% of a political 
subdivision’s total revenue for that fiscal year, after deducting 
program costs, must be remitted to the general fund.
• Pennsylvania law provides that Philadelphia may not collect equal to 
or greater than 2% of its annual budget in fine revenue from its speed 
camera use in specified corridors and school zones.
Virginia State Crime Commission
27

=====PAGE 28=====
Revenue Uses
• Virginia’s statute does not limit how localities spend revenue from 
civil penalties in school zones and work zones.
• Staff sent an informal survey to localities inquiring about speed 
camera revenue use which found that some allocate revenue to 
their general fund, while others fund specific line items, such as 
speed camera operating expenses, traffic safety initiatives, and 
law-enforcement personnel and equipment.
Virginia State Crime Commission

Revenue Uses
• Other states vary in how revenue can be allocated:
Cannot supplant existing expenditures;
Administrative costs;
Highway safety infrastructure and education;
Highway safety enforcement and training;
School programs; or,
State general fund.

=====PAGE 30=====
Collections: In-State
• Virginia’s statute does not penalize individuals for failure to pay 
the civil penalty.
A separate Virginia statute allows for in-state collection of civil 
penalties assessed by a court.
• At least 4 states refuse to register or renew a vehicle registration 
if the owner has an unpaid speed camera penalty.
• One state suspends a person’s driver’s license for nonpayment.
Virginia State Crime Commission
30

=====PAGE 31=====
Collections: Out-of-State
• Virginia’s statute allows for collections of civil penalties against out-
of- state drivers, but this poses challenges due to the Driver License 
Compact.
45 states, including Virginia, and D.C., are part of the Compact.
• The Drivers License Compact does not apply to speed camera 
violations as set forth in the Virginia Code.
Compact states share information on convictions; however, a 
conviction is defined as a motor vehicle offense prohibited by state law 
which is required to be reported to the licensing authority.
A Virginia speed camera violation is not a conviction and is not 
reported to the Virginia DMV.
Virginia State Crime Commission
31

=====PAGE 32=====
Device Placement
• Virginia is consistent with other states in the placement of speed 
cameras in school zones (18 other states) and work zones (15 
other states).
• Some states allow for use in other specific locations, such as:
Pedestrian zones;
Residential districts;
Parks;
Safety corridors or high-risk crash areas;
Colleges and universities; or,
Hospitals.
• 4 states and D.C. do not restrict use to specific locations.
Virginia State Crime Commission
32

=====PAGE 33=====
Device Placement
• Virginia’s statute does not include any other placement limits.
• Other states have adopted measures to restrict placement:
Requiring other traffic calming measures be adopted first;
Limiting the number of devices that can be deployed; or,
Mandating equitable disbursement of devices.
Virginia State Crime Commission
33

=====PAGE 34=====
Signage Distance
• Per Virginia’s statute, “[a] conspicuous sign shall be placed within 
1,000 feet of any school crossing zone, highway work zone, or 
high-risk intersection segment at which a photo speed 
monitoring device is used, indicating the use of the device.”
This language could allow signage to be placed within less than 1,000 
feet of, or even after, a location where a speed camera is in use.
• Virginia’s statute could be amended to require signage be placed 
at least 1,000 feet before a location where a camera is in use.
Virginia State Crime Commission
34

=====PAGE 35=====
Speed Feedback Signs
• Virginia’s statute does not require the placement of speed 
feedback signs before a speed camera zone.
• Research shows that speed feedback signs are an effective 
measure to reduce speed.
• Virginia could require that speed feedback signs be placed 
before a location where a speed camera is in use.
Virginia State Crime Commission
35

=====PAGE 36=====
Evaluation of Effectiveness
• Virginia’s statute does not require any evaluations to assess the 
need or effectiveness of a speed camera in a specific location.
• Other states require evaluations, such as:
Initial speed study prior to placement;
Periodic speed studies after placement; or,
Safety data analysis.
Virginia State Crime Commission
36

=====PAGE 37=====
Public Awareness
• Virginia’s statute does not require any public awareness 
measures related to the use of speed cameras.
• Other Virginia statutes require public awareness, such as:
Red light cameras: public awareness program plus making 
monthly system function evaluations available to the public; and,
ALPR: public awareness program plus the public posting of use 
policies and reports.
Virginia State Crime Commission

=====PAGE 38=====
Vendor Approval Process
• Virginia’s statute allows law enforcement agencies to contract 
with private vendors, but does not include a vendor approval 
process.
The Division of General Services (DGS) determines the proper 
equipment for speed camera use, but not the individual vendor.
• Virginia’s ALPR statute requires vendors to certify to DGS that 
their systems and practices meet statutory criteria.
Virginia State Crime Commission
38

=====PAGE 39=====
Privacy
• Virginia’s statute includes privacy protections such as:
Limiting data collection to what is needed to enforce the statute;
Restricting the sharing of data; and,
Destruction of data after 60 days of paying the civil penalty.
• Other states have adopted additional privacy measures, such as:
Devices cannot be used for surveillance (e.g. ALPR); or,
Cameras cannot capture images of drivers or occupants.
Virginia State Crime Commission
39

=====PAGE 40=====
Calibration
• Virginia requires speed cameras to be calibrated annually.
Speed equipment used by Virginia law enforcement must be 
calibrated every 6 months, and officers generally test this 
equipment at the beginning and end of each shift.
• Other states impose various calibration requirements:
Annual or semiannual calibration by independent lab;
Monthly or bimonthly calibration by trained technician; or,
Daily internal calibration.
Virginia State Crime Commission
40

=====PAGE 41=====
Annual Report
• Agencies using speed cameras must annually report certain data to 
VSP by January 15th. 
• VSP must annually publish an aggregated report by February 15th.
• Virginia’s statute could be amended to require earlier reporting 
dates so that information is available prior to Session.
Virginia State Crime Commission
41

=====PAGE 42=====
Annual Report
• Virginia’s statute only requires annual reporting on traffic 
violations prosecuted, successful prosecutions, and civil 
penalties collected.
• Some states have additional reporting requirements, such as:
Safety objectives and outcomes;
Gross and net revenue;
Use of revenue;
Administrative costs;
Violations categorized by speed, repeat offenders, or state license 
plate of the offending vehicle; or,
Number of violations rejected and the reasons.
Virginia State Crime Commission

=====PAGE 44=====
Automated Enforcement
• Speed cameras are just one form of automated enforcement.
• The Virginia Code also allows automated enforcement for:
Red light violations;
Toll violations; and,
Passing a stopped school bus.
• The Virginia statutes that regulate the use of these technologies 
are not aligned. 
Virginia State Crime Commission
44

=====PAGE 45=====
Automated Enforcement
• Technology to conduct automated enforcement of other 
violations is rapidly growing, such as:
Stop signs;
Pedestrian crossings;
Parking;
Illegal turns;
Bus lanes; and,
Oversized vehicles.
• Additionally, autonomous vehicles are now being deployed.
Virginia State Crime Commission
45

=====PAGE 46=====
Automated Enforcement
• Virginia could prohibit the use of automated enforcement 
technology that is not specifically authorized by statute to:
Provide time to align the current statutory framework; and,
Ensure that new technology can be regulated.
• Legislation related to automated enforcement technology 
could be referred to the Crime Commission for review.
Virginia State Crime Commission
