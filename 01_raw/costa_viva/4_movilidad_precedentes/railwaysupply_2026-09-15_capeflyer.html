CapeFLYER ridership climbs 7% with three weekly round trips in 2026

By Oleksandr Batrak

Published September 15, 2026

In the United States, CapeFLYER ridership reached a record 16,045 passengers on weekend Boston–Hyannis trains in summer 2026, 7 per cent above the 2025 season.

The seasonal service connects Boston with Hyannis and operates mainly on weekends between Memorial Day and Labor Day, with expanded holiday service.

The MBTA and the Cape Cod Regional Transit Authority (CCRTA) announced the figure on 10 September and described it as a record. Keolis, the MBTA’s Commuter Rail operations and maintenance partner, operates the trains. MassDOT is the fourth member of the service partnership.

Service provided one round trip on each operating day

The 2026 timetable provided one round trip on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays after the season opened on 22 May, together with expanded holiday service. The MBTA also specifically confirmed regular service on Friday, 19 June, during a World Cup match at Boston Stadium. The stopping pattern stayed familiar too. Braintree, Brockton, Lakeville, Wareham, Buzzards Bay and Bourne lie between the two terminals, and the MBTA puts the trip at about two hours and forty minutes.

Two records counted in different ways

Comparison with earlier seasons needs care because the releases use inconsistent measures. In September 2025, the MBTA reported more than 10,800 tickets sold, compared with about 9,700 in 2024. The same release also referred to the buyers as passengers and described the result as record ridership. The 2026 announcement instead reported 16,045 passengers carried and a 7 per cent year-on-year increase. It does not provide an exact 2025 passenger total directly comparable with the 2026 figure.

Both counts nevertheless point the same way. CCRTA administrator John Kennedy put the previous season’s ridership growth at 9 per cent, when the operators confirmed the 2026 timetable in May. Keolis said at the same point that more than 10,000 tickets had been sold during 2025.

Weekend leisure traffic on the Boston network

Officials tied the result to travellers avoiding queues at the canal crossings. Kennedy also called the train a fixture of summer on the Cape. Keolis general manager and chief executive John Killeen framed the season as a network-wide trend:

“The Commuter Rail continues to attract more leisure riders than ever before, and we’re looking forward to seeing passengers on weekend trains this fall.”

Fares meanwhile stayed straightforward. A round trip between South Station and Hyannis cost $40 this season. Children aged 11 and under travelled free alongside a paying adult. The consist also included a bike car with cycle racks and a tool kit, along with a café car. The CCRTA adds that its buses carry arriving passengers around the Cape at no charge.

Cape Cod Bridges Program clears environmental review

Those bridge queues form on two road crossings that are now moving toward replacement. MassDOT reports a certificate from the Massachusetts Environmental Policy Act Office on the Final Environmental Impact Report in April 2026. The Federal Highway Administration then approved the Final Environmental Impact Statement and issued a Record of Decision in June 2026. Foundation load testing for the future Sagamore Bridge has since begun in Bourne, at four locations north and south of the canal. The programme covers the Sagamore and Bourne road crossings, so the railway route to Hyannis sits outside its scope.
