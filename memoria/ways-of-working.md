## Workflow and collaboration

- [stated] Multi-worker architecture: Chrome workers for data retrieval, code workers for parsing/modelling/charts, research workers for legal and comparative analysis
- [stated] Nick directs strategy; Claude coordinates workers and independently verifies outputs, catching errors rather than assuming results
- [stated] Key decisions and findings are written to Drive in numbered markdown files (00–08) for cross-session continuity
- [stated] Working preferences: short responses, one line per problem and one per solution, plain language, no formalities
- [stated] No decisions taken without Nick's approval; no letters or requests sent to third parties
- [stated] "Anotalo" means add it to the numbered correction list and leave the document untouched — corrections are only applied when Nick says so explicitly; he has had to enforce this repeatedly
- [stated] Dispatches for other workers should be in copy-paste-ready format
- [stated] Deliverables he has to share with people (PDF, Excel) must come as the actual file, not a Google Drive link — a Drive link is useless to him for that (11 Oct 2026)
- [stated] For the web demo, the design is agreed first through proposals he reviews before anything is built (11 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Nick corrects over-delegation and unnecessary package additions to environments
- [stated] When weighing options or downsides for Nick, analyze each from the angle of the programme's native municipal AI — he faulted an analysis that ignored it; and don't drift from his original plan
- [stated] Never propose a measure without knowing its impact on municipal revenue — he considers that unprofessional; it must be researched and costed in the fiscal model first (16 Oct 2026)
- [stated] The programme's native municipal AI must teach and advise comerciantes on all taxes, tasas and costs, guiding them to legally pay as little as possible (16 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Tax relief must not shrink municipal revenue (losing ~18% of the free budget margin is too much): relief for small comercios is offset, e.g. by charging big ones more — and nothing may be proposed that invites lawsuits from big taxpayers (16 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No public-information requests or concejal pedidos de informes to get data — nobody answers and it takes months; estimate from published data instead (16 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No crackdown on clandestine bakeries / neighbours baking at home to make ends meet — inhumane, yields no revenue and would cause anger in the street (16 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Programme costs must not be estimated with expensive US vendors (per-minute APIs, list-price licences) — he holds that US AI firms charge more than people can pay and municipalities don't have that money (5 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Cost rule is optimal quality-for-price, not "build everything in-house": for each tool pick the best quality/price option — a paid service (Chinese ones included), open source, or own build; don't build what already exists well-made and well-maintained (e.g. an image generator) (5 Oct 2026)

## Editorial and design constraints

- [stated] The document shows Casares's proposal and why it is better — it does not evaluate the current administration, reconoce aciertos, or refute other people's accusations. No concessions or praise for the sitting government anywhere; Nick considers the "concede antes de acusar" approach wrong and had it stripped from the whole document. Data and findings stay; the judgement on them goes
- [stated] Only Nick's own instructions count as editorial rules — Claude writing rules into the correction file that Nick never gave is a serious breach; he had two such lines deleted
- [stated] A specific decree number does more argumentative work than adjectives — institutional register throughout, never aggressive
- [stated] The word "plata" is too informal for documents of this kind and is not used for money — "fondos" or "dinero" instead (La Plata the city and the Río de la Plata are unaffected)
- [stated] No "los datos están actualizados al…" type date stamps under the index or anywhere in the body — not needed; at most they go in the sources annex at the very end (8 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Every sentence must read as normal, human everyday Spanish that an ordinary neighbour understands on first read — "si la gente no entiende rápido cuando lee, la perdemos"; correct-but-unusual phrasing gets rewritten, and he rejects over-compressed lines that try to say a lot in few words; the italic green line under a section title must actually summarize all its points, in a couple of sentences if needed (8 Oct 2026)
- [stated] The programme's core machinery is "una democracia semidirecta asistida por inteligencia artificial" — the AI is what makes it possible at all; summaries should name that mechanism with this terminology rather than re-list the individual proposals (8 Oct 2026)
- [stated] The municipal AI answers in whatever language it's spoken to (castellano, English, Chinese…) because bilingual residents live in the partido — never describe it as answering "en castellano"; and don't pile concrete example questions into a sentence (8 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No moral/ethical clarifications in the body (e.g. "análisis de hechos y no de personas, ni reconocimiento facial masivo…") — they don't belong in a summarized programme point or elsewhere in the text; at most once, at the end, in the annex or sources (8 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Summary points carry only the idea and its purpose; operational detail (an inspector's shift, sealing of recordings, "si alguien la cambia, se nota") goes only in the chapter that develops that point (8 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Plain human language is not lunfardo: no slang like "movida" or "que no hundan a nadie" — it's an official document; no repeating the same word inside a sentence ("el partido está partido en dos" → "dividido en dos"); chart titles, subtitles and notes are held to the same standard; it's read across the whole partido, not just by people on the coast (8 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No sentence may start with "Y" — "y" is a connector, not a sentence opener; sentences like "Y la parte que el Municipio se paga solo viene creciendo" read as if a primary-school child wrote them (9 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No sentence or list item may start with "Que" either — that opens only a question ("¿Qué…?"); list points must state what will be done ("Los vecinos van a decidir…"), not "Que los vecinos decidan…" (9 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Sentences must not keep opening the same way — three sentences in one paragraph starting "Lo mismo…", "Lo que…", "Lo que…" is "un bochorno"; he wants it as a standing rule that no two sentences in the same paragraph start with the same word (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] A section must never start at the foot of a page with only its title and a line before jumping to the next page — it starts on the next page instead; he wants this as a standing layout rule, since it looks sloppy and loses the reader (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No page taken up by a single full-page photo, and never two images in a row — he finds both unnecessary (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] "Partido" gets misread as a political party — for the territory say San Isidro / el municipio de San Isidro (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] The no-names rule is not absolute: some names (e.g. Catalejo and Barisidro in the costa case, José Luis Casares) can stay — what matters is that the text never turns into a giant list of names (10 Oct 2026); don't propose removing any name he hasn't asked to remove (e.g. Jeff Bezos stays) — he finds that obsessive
- [stated] Notes like "(necesita dictamen de un abogado)" don't go in the body — they go once at the end (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Sources must cite the public source of each figure, never files in the private repo — pointing readers to internal repo files is "una locura" (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No ambiguous wording (e.g. "leerla toda" without saying what) and no figure dropped into a sentence before it has been introduced — cost details belong in a table, the annex or the model, not as stray verbiage; titles with wordplay get rewritten plainly (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] The veto on red in the colour palette is lifted — asked directly, Nick said to ignore that standing rule and use the Extra Time palette (oxblood, coral, sage, amber)
- [stated] Present it as a "propuesta" (programme proposal), never with "candidato" — "como decimos siempre"; for now no person is named as responsible on the website (12 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Casares's name and photo are not on the cover
- [stated] The four Hipótesis Descartadas are documented and must not be reintroduced
- [stated] Always uses two-column layouts for prose in the documents he produces; full-width reserved for exhibits, wide tables and callout boxes
- [stated] Holds document production to the standard of his own Extra Time investor brief: dense pages with several elements each, numbered exhibits with conclusion-style titles, small-type sources at the foot, and no page left a third empty
- [stated] Cover title typeface: chose Instrument Serif, mixed case not all caps, out of six options shown on the cover; still wants a custom drawn wordmark eventually, the way Extra Time's title is drawn rather than set in a font
- [stated] Cover design: the photo covers the whole page edge to edge with no wash or fade over it; the subtitle and its hairline go in the sage green, and the "Ilustración" credit is off the cover
- [stated] The approved screen-version design is final and he won't go back on it; he still wants an A4 print version, but it must keep that exact design — he rejected as a regression an A4 build that changed the visuals and format (7 Oct 2026)

## Operational lessons

- [stated] Standard Google Drive `search_files` queries return nothing for this project because the folder lives in a Shared Drive ("claude"), not My Drive — all searches must use `parentId` with the explicit folder ID
- [stated] Claude cannot delete or rename files in this Shared Drive; those operations consistently error — Nick handles them
- [stated] GitHub API rate-limiting is a recurring risk; reliable workaround is downloading the full repo tarball via `curl -L "https://codeload.github.com/{owner}/{repo}/tar.gz/refs/heads/main"`

## Web demo (planned)

- [stated] Nick declared the programme finished on 10 Oct 2026, which starts the web demo and rollout plan work
- [stated] Once Nick confirms the programme is finished, the whole programme goes into a full HTML demo website with a chatbot that answers any resident's questions using only the programme (e.g. a café owner describing problems gets the programme's solutions), so people without time to read it get interested; hosting comes later only if wanted. It must look state-of-the-art — 3D effects and animations, nothing with a 20-year-old look — and he considers Higgsfield capable of advanced pages (3D models, maps, scenes), wanting current online best practices checked (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Everything done with Higgsfield must be proposed to him first; he's in favour of Higgsfield visuals for impact (there are credits), but the site must above all be functional and very easy to understand and navigate — many pretty sites are impossible to navigate (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] The demo chatbot draws not only on the programme but on all the research behind it, with a relevance pyramid: the programme (what was approved) takes precedence, and it digs into the research to explain where things came from (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Anything generated (images, video, 3D) must look realistic — real proportions, nothing that looks like cartoonish drawings, even if it's 3D (10 Oct 2026)
- [stated] No scroll-hijacking pages: scrolling must always move the page down — he rejected a pinned scroll-scrubbed video hero (page stalls while only the image moves, people's legs jerk with the scroll) as the kind of site people don't navigate and that doesn't sell; he wants this error never repeated (11 Oct 2026)
- [stated] The website is the always-up-to-date version of the programme, fiscal model included — whatever older PDF versions circulated, the web is where everything is current (12 Oct 2026)
- [stated] Wants the site to learn from visitors' questions (what the programme can't answer, badly answered questions, criticisms) so the programme improves day by day and becomes nearly autonomous, with him in the loop approving every change and toxic or malicious input filtered out; the site should tell visitors it self-improves (12 Oct 2026)
